Sushi Nutrition Calculator

Sushi calories are easier to estimate when you know what is inside the roll and how much you actually ate .

Two restaurants can use the same name, such as California Roll, Dragon Roll, or Philadelphia Roll, while using different amounts of sushi rice, fish, avocado, cream cheese, mayonnaise, tempura, and sauce. Piece size can also vary.

Use the Sushi Nutrition Calculator above to estimate your meal by choosing the exact roll, nigiri, sashimi, sides, sauces, and number of pieces you ate.

The values on this page should be treated as standardized estimates , not universal nutrition facts for every sushi restaurant.

Start With the Type of Sushi

The first choice should be the format:

Maki Roll

Specialty Roll

Nigiri

Sashimi

or:

Sides & Condiments .

These foods use very different serving structures.

A six-piece roll contains sushi rice plus fillings.

Nigiri combines a slice of seafood or another topping with a mound of sushi rice.

Sashimi is normally served without rice.

Because the rice is one of the main sources of carbohydrate in sushi, those formats should not share one generic serving calculation.

Piece Count Is Not Enough by Itself

A six-piece roll at one restaurant may weigh much more than a six-piece roll somewhere else.

The same applies to nigiri and sashimi.

That means:

6 pieces

is useful information, but:

6 pieces + total serving weight

is considerably better.

When a verified weight is available, NutritionSolver should show both.

For example:

California Roll, 6 pieces, estimated 170g

is more useful than:

California Roll, 6 pieces

with no recipe or weight context.

California Roll Calories Can Vary by Recipe

NutritionSolver currently estimates a six-piece California Roll at approximately:

255 calories

9g protein

38g carbohydrates

7g fat

and:

690mg sodium .

That can be a reasonable reference estimate, but it should not be presented as the calorie count for every California Roll.

Published food-composition examples differ substantially because serving weight and recipe differ. One USDA-derived database example lists a six-piece 170g California Roll around 160 calories, while another university dining recipe contains a much larger serving with substantially more rice and other ingredients.

The better label is:

Estimated California Roll, Standard Recipe

with the assumed weight and ingredients visible.

A California Roll Needs Its Ingredients Defined

A typical California-style roll may contain:

sushi rice

nori

imitation crab or crab mixture

avocado

cucumber

and sometimes:

mayonnaise or sesame .

Changing imitation crab to a mayonnaise-heavy crab salad can change calories, fat, and sodium.

Adding more avocado changes the result again.

The calculator should eventually let users choose the recipe style rather than pretending all California Rolls are identical.

Spicy Tuna Is Different From a Plain Tuna Roll

NutritionSolver currently estimates:

Tuna Roll, 6 pieces: 220 calories

and:

Spicy Tuna Roll, 6 pieces: 290 calories .

That difference makes sense conceptually because spicy tuna recipes commonly include additional sauce or mayonnaise.

The important point is that spicy tuna cannot be calculated as plain tuna plus hot sauce alone unless that is actually how the restaurant prepares it.

Some restaurants use mayonnaise-heavy spicy tuna mixtures, while others use much less.

The sauce amount belongs in the recipe.

Spicy Salmon Has the Same Problem

A plain salmon roll, salmon-avocado roll, and spicy salmon roll should all have different recipe models.

NutritionSolver currently estimates its six-piece Spicy Salmon Roll at approximately:

305 calories

while its Salmon Avocado Roll is approximately:

300 calories .

Those figures can be useful standard estimates, but the actual ranking can change depending on how much avocado, salmon, rice, and spicy mayonnaise a restaurant uses.

This is another reason to show:

Estimated

rather than:

Exact .

Avocado Changes Both Calories and Fat

Avocado contributes considerably more energy than cucumber or many other vegetable fillings.

NutritionSolver currently estimates:

Cucumber Roll, 6 pieces: 130 calories

and:

Avocado Roll, 6 pieces: 225 calories .

That illustrates how two meat-free sushi rolls can have very different nutrition.

When avocado is also placed on top of a specialty roll, it should be included again according to the recipe amount.

Philadelphia Roll Needs a Defined Cream Cheese Portion

NutritionSolver currently estimates a six-piece Philadelphia Roll at:

285 calories

12g protein

32g carbohydrates

and:

11g fat .

But Philadelphia Roll recipes vary significantly.

A commercially packaged eight-piece Philadelphia Roll can contain around 210 calories , while other published versions use different quantities of smoked salmon, cream cheese, rice, and seasonings.

Therefore the calculator should define:

fish type

cream cheese amount

rice weight

and:

piece count

for the reference recipe.

Specialty Roll Names Are Not Standard Recipes

Names such as:

Dragon Roll

Tiger Roll

Volcano Roll

Caterpillar Roll

and:

Rainbow Roll

do not describe one internationally standardized recipe.

One restaurant's Dragon Roll can be made with eel and avocado.

Another can use shrimp tempura, crab, avocado, eel sauce, spicy mayonnaise, or different toppings.

The name alone is not enough to guarantee calories or macros.

NutritionSolver should therefore attach a visible ingredient description to every specialty-roll estimate.

Dragon Roll Should Show the Reference Recipe

NutritionSolver currently estimates its eight-piece Dragon Roll at approximately:

470 calories

14g protein

58g carbohydrates

21g fat

and:

1,100mg sodium .

That value should be paired with something like:

Reference recipe: [actual ingredients used for this estimate]

Without that recipe, a user cannot know whether the Dragon Roll at their restaurant resembles NutritionSolver's version.

Rainbow Roll Depends on the Fish Selection

A Rainbow Roll commonly combines a California-style base with several seafood toppings.

The exact fish can differ.

One published university recipe, for example, includes tuna, crab, yellowtail, salmon, avocado, cucumber, mayonnaise, sushi rice, and other ingredients and contains 441 calories for its specific serving.

NutritionSolver currently estimates its eight-piece Rainbow Roll at:

410 calories

and:

22g protein .

Those values are plausible as a standardized estimate, but the ingredient list and serving weight need to accompany them.

Tempura Needs to Be Part of the Recipe

NutritionSolver currently estimates an eight-piece Shrimp Tempura Roll at:

500 calories

16g protein

64g carbohydrates

18g fat

and:

1,200mg sodium .

A tempura roll can contain calories from:

sushi rice

fried shrimp or vegetables

tempura batter

oil absorbed during frying

avocado

and:

sauces or toppings .

Do not calculate it as plain shrimp sushi.

The fried component is part of the finished recipe.

Spicy Mayo Needs Its Own Calculator Component

Spicy mayonnaise is one of the most useful missing components for a generic sushi calculator.

It can appear:

inside a spicy tuna roll

inside spicy salmon

on top of specialty rolls

or:

as an extra dipping sauce .

NutritionSolver should allow users to add a measured amount such as:

1 teaspoon

1 tablespoon

or:

restaurant serving

when a reliable recipe is available.

This would make specialty-roll estimates much more flexible.

Eel Sauce Should Also Be Separate

Sweet eel-style sauces can contribute carbohydrates, sugar, and sodium even when the roll itself does not contain a large amount.

A specialty roll with eel sauce should therefore not use the same nutrition as the same roll without the sauce.

Add:

Eel Sauce / Unagi Sauce

as a selectable component with an explicit serving amount.

Nigiri Needs Fish Plus Rice

NutritionSolver currently includes six two-piece nigiri estimates.

Examples include approximately:

Tuna Nigiri: 120 calories

Salmon Nigiri: 140

Shrimp Nigiri: 110

Eel Nigiri: 175

Yellowtail Nigiri: 140

Tamago Nigiri: 130.

These should remain clearly labeled:

2 pieces .

The rice beneath the topping is part of the nutrition.

That is what separates nigiri from sashimi.

Nigiri Rice Weight Should Be a Variable

Even two pieces of salmon nigiri can differ if one sushi chef uses much larger rice portions.

A better nutrition model would store:

fish grams per piece

and:

seasoned sushi rice grams per piece .

Then the user could calculate two, four, six, or another number of pieces without relying on a fixed restaurant-sized portion.

Sashimi Needs Weight More Than Piece Count

Sashimi does not normally include sushi rice, which makes its nutrition simpler to estimate.

But the weight of each slice still matters.

NutritionSolver currently estimates three-piece portions including approximately:

Salmon Sashimi: 95 calories

Tuna Sashimi: 70

Yellowtail Sashimi: 105

Octopus Sashimi: 60 .

The calculator should display the assumed gram weight for those three pieces.

Three thin salmon slices and three thick salmon slices cannot reasonably be expected to contain exactly the same nutrition.

Salmon and Tuna Should Use Different Food References

Salmon and tuna have different fat profiles.

NutritionSolver already reflects this by giving its salmon sashimi estimate more fat than its tuna estimate.

That distinction should remain.

The next improvement is identifying the actual reference fish used, for example the species or generic USDA food record used to calculate the estimate.

This is especially important for salmon because wild and farmed salmon can differ in fat and calorie density.

Sushi Rice Is Not the Same as Plain Steamed Rice

NutritionSolver currently includes:

Steamed White Rice, 1 cup: 205 calories .

That can be useful as a side dish.

However, sushi rice used inside rolls and nigiri is normally seasoned.

The recipe can include:

rice vinegar

sugar

and:

salt .

Do not automatically use plain steamed-rice nutrition as the sushi-rice component of every roll.

Create a separate:

Seasoned Sushi Rice

ingredient with a documented recipe.

Rice Amount Is One of the Most Important Inputs

When users estimate sushi, they often focus on the fish.

But a maki roll or nigiri serving can contain a substantial amount of rice.

For a custom-roll builder, allow the user to choose or estimate:

light rice

standard rice

extra rice

or preferably an actual:

rice weight in grams .

This would improve both calorie and carbohydrate estimates.

Soy Sauce Adds Few Calories but a Large Amount of Sodium

NutritionSolver currently lists one tablespoon of soy sauce at approximately:

10 calories

and:

920mg sodium .

USDA-based nutrition references put a tablespoon of soy sauce at roughly 8 to 9 calories and about 879mg sodium, so the current NutritionSolver estimate is broadly consistent with the expected range.

The more important number for soy sauce is therefore usually sodium, not calories.

Soy Sauce Should Be Measured by Amount Used

A restaurant may place a large dish or several packets of soy sauce on the table, but that does not mean the diner consumes all of it.

Let users select:

1 teaspoon

1 tablespoon

2 tablespoons

or:

custom amount .

Also create a separate record for:

Reduced-Sodium Soy Sauce

when a validated source is available.

Pickled Ginger Is a Condiment, Not a Meal

NutritionSolver currently estimates one ounce of Pickled Ginger at:

20 calories .

That can stay as a condiment component.

But it should not be compared directly with an entire Shrimp Tempura Roll in “lightest foods” rankings.

Different serving types need different comparison groups.

Sides Need Their Serving Sizes

NutritionSolver currently includes:

Edamame, 1 cup

Miso Soup, 1 bowl

Seaweed Salad

Gyoza, 6 pieces

Steamed White Rice, 1 cup

Soy Sauce, 1 tablespoon

and:

Pickled Ginger, 1 ounce .

The first improvement is consistency.

Every side should display an actual serving unit.

Seaweed Salad should say:

grams, ounces, cups, or restaurant serving .

Miso Soup should identify the assumed fluid-ounce or gram serving rather than simply “1 bowl.”

Restaurant-Specific Nutrition Should Override Generic Estimates

If a sushi restaurant publishes its own nutrition facts, use those values instead of the generic calculator.

Restaurant recipes can use very different:

rice portions

fish weights

tempura batter

cream cheese

mayonnaise

sauces

and:

piece sizes .

The generic Sushi Nutrition Calculator is most useful when restaurant-specific nutrition is unavailable.

Build the Sushi Meal From the Pieces You Actually Ate

For a roll , choose:

Roll type → Piece count → Rice amount → Fillings → Toppings → Tempura → Sauces

For nigiri :

Fish or topping → Piece count → Rice amount

For sashimi :

Fish → Piece count or grams

Then add:

Miso Soup → Edamame → Gyoza → Seaweed Salad → Soy Sauce → Pickled Ginger

The most useful sushi calculation is not a claim that every Dragon Roll or California Roll has one exact nutrition profile. It is a transparent estimate built from a defined serving and recipe.

When a restaurant publishes nutrition for its own sushi, use that restaurant-specific value first. When it does not, NutritionSolver can provide a standardized reference estimate with the ingredients, serving weight, and methodology clearly shown.
