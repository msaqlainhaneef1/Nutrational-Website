Costa Vida Nutrition Calculator

Costa Vida meals are built from several separate ingredients, so the final nutrition depends on much more than whether you order a burrito, salad, taco, or Baja Bowl.

Your portion size, tortilla, rice, beans, protein, sauce, cheese, dressing, and toppings all contribute to the final calories and macros.

Use the Costa Vida Nutrition Calculator above to recreate your order ingredient by ingredient and see the combined calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, and other available nutrition values.

Regular and Light Portions Are Not the Same

Costa Vida gives customers the option to choose regular or light portions for several entrées.

That distinction matters when calculating nutrition.

A light salad, for example, uses smaller portions of several ingredients than a regular salad. The same food may therefore have different nutrition values depending on where and how it is used.

Always select the serving that matches your actual entrée rather than assuming one ingredient value applies to every Costa Vida meal.

Sweet Pork Has Its Own Nutrition Profile

Costa Vida's Sweet Pork is one of the brand's signature proteins.

Unlike plain grilled meat, Sweet Pork gets some of its calories from carbohydrates as well as protein and fat.

The current Costa Vida nutrition calculator lists a regular 4 oz serving at approximately:

290 calories
13g protein
21g carbohydrates
17g fat

By comparison, a standard 4 oz serving of Grilled Chicken is about 190 calories with 29g of protein .

This is why choosing a protein changes more than just the protein number.

A Costa Vida Burrito Includes Much More Than a Tortilla

Costa Vida burritos are filled with meat, cheese, rice, beans, and sauce, then smothered with additional sauce and cheese.

The tortilla itself already contributes a meaningful amount. The current official nutrition calculator lists the 12-inch flour tortilla at 480 calories .

From there, rice, beans, protein, sauce, and cheese are added.

For example:

12-inch tortilla + rice + beans + Sweet Pork + sauce + cheese

can become a substantially different meal from the same burrito made with grilled chicken and a lighter sauce.

Costa Vida's current menu lists burritos across a broad 690 to 1,490 calorie range , which shows how much the final combination can matter.

The Sauce You Choose Can Shift the Total

Costa Vida offers several sauces with very different nutrition profiles.

For a regular burrito portion, current values include approximately:

Tomatillo Cilantro Sauce: 60 calories

Red Enchilada Sauce: 120 calories

Raspberry Chipotle Sauce: 120 calories

Roasted Green Chile Sauce: 130 calories

The carbohydrate and fat values also differ between sauces.

This makes sauce choice especially important on smothered burritos and enchiladas, where the serving can be larger than a small salsa portion.

Salad Dressing Deserves Its Own Calculation

A Costa Vida salad includes much more than lettuce.

A standard salad can contain protein, rice, beans, tortilla, pico de gallo, guacamole, tortilla strips, cotija cheese, and dressing.

The dressing alone can add a meaningful amount to the total.

Current 2 oz dressing values include approximately:

Tomatillo Ranch: 170 calories

Cilantro Lime Vinaigrette: 180 calories

Mango Dressing: 160 calories

If you use the full serving, include it in your calculation. If you use only part of the dressing, your actual intake will be lower than the complete portion.

Baja Bowls Remove the Large Burrito Tortilla

Costa Vida describes its Baja Bowl as a lighter way to enjoy burrito-style ingredients.

It contains components such as meat, cheese, rice, beans, sauce, lettuce, and pico de gallo, but it does not start with the large 12-inch flour tortilla used for a burrito.

That removes a major calorie and carbohydrate component.

However, a Baja Bowl is not automatically low in calories. Sweet Pork, cheese, rice, beans, guacamole, and sauces can still add substantially to the total.

The ingredient combination remains more important than the word “bowl.”

Rice and Beans Should Be Added Separately

Costa Vida's Cilantro Lime Rice and beans each contribute independently to the meal.

For regular entrée portions, the current calculator lists:

Cilantro Lime Rice, 4 oz: 140 calories

Black Beans, 3 oz: 80 calories

Pinto Beans, 3 oz: 80 calories

Refried Beans, 3 oz: 120 calories

Beans also contribute protein, while refried beans contain more fat than the black or pinto bean servings shown.

If you order both rice and beans, include both in your total.

Taco Nutrition Depends on Tortilla and Quantity

Costa Vida tacos can use either corn or flour tortillas, and single and double taco servings use different quantities of protein and other ingredients.

A current 6-inch corn tortilla is approximately 120 calories , while a 6-inch flour tortilla is about 160 calories .

If you order two tacos, make sure the calculation reflects two tortillas and the larger protein portion rather than simply doubling an unrelated single-taco total.

Quesadillas Can Be Calorie-Dense Meals

Costa Vida's current menu lists quesadillas in a range of approximately 1,115 to 1,465 calories depending on the preparation.

That is useful context because a quesadilla may sound simpler than a multi-component burrito, but the combination of a large tortilla, cheese, protein, and accompanying ingredients can produce a substantial meal.

Use the exact quesadilla and protein choice rather than estimating it from another entrée.

Chips, Queso, Guacamole, and Drinks Count Separately

Extras are easy to overlook when calculating restaurant nutrition.

The current Costa Vida calculator lists a 2.5 oz serving of tortilla chips at approximately 360 calories before queso, guacamole, or another dip is added.

For a complete order, calculate:

Entrée + chips or side + dip + drink + dessert

instead of stopping at the burrito or bowl.
