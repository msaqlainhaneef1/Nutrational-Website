What Is Body Fat Percentage?

Body fat percentage tells you how much of your total body weight is made up of fat. Unlike body weight alone, it gives you a clearer picture of body composition by separating body fat from lean mass such as muscle, bone, and other tissues.

Use the Body Fat Calculator above to estimate your body fat percentage from simple circumference measurements.

How Does the Body Fat Calculator Work?

This calculator uses the U.S. Navy body fat method , which estimates body fat percentage from your height and body measurements.

For men, the calculation uses:

  • Height
  • Neck circumference
  • Waist circumference

For women, it also includes hip circumference .

The method uses the relationship between these measurements to estimate body composition without requiring calipers, a smart scale, or a body scan.

How to Measure for Body Fat Percentage

Accurate measurements help produce a more consistent estimate.

Measure your neck just below the larynx and keep the tape comfortably against the skin without pulling it tight. Measure your waist around the navel while standing relaxed.

Women should also measure the hips at the widest point .

Try to take each measurement under similar conditions every time. Using the same measuring technique is especially useful if you are tracking changes in body fat over time.

What Does Your Body Fat Result Mean?

Your result is an estimated percentage of your body weight that comes from fat .

For example, a result of 20% body fat means the calculator estimates that approximately 20% of your total body weight is fat mass, while the remaining weight comes from lean tissue and other body components.

Body fat percentage can provide useful context that body weight or BMI alone cannot show because people with similar height and weight can have very different body compositions.

How Accurate Is the U.S. Navy Body Fat Method?

The U.S. Navy method provides a practical body fat estimate , but it is not a direct measurement.

Small differences in where or how you place the measuring tape can change the result. Individual differences in body shape and fat distribution can also affect the estimate.

For this reason, it is better to use the result as a reference point rather than an exact measurement. Using the same method consistently can also make it easier to follow changes over time.
