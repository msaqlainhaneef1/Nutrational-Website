Firehouse Subs Nutrition Calculator

At Firehouse Subs, the sandwich name is only part of the nutrition calculation.

The same sub can be ordered in different sizes, while bread, cheese, mayo, sauces, bacon, vegetables, and other customizations can change the calories and macros further. Adding chips, fries, soup, a cookie, or a drink turns the sandwich into a much larger meal.

Use the Firehouse Subs Nutrition Calculator above to select the exact sub and size you order, compare nutrition between sandwiches, and combine your entrée with sides and extras.

Small, Medium, and Large Are Very Different Servings

Sub size is one of the most important choices at Firehouse Subs.

A Small, Medium, and Large version of the same sandwich contain different amounts of bread, meat, cheese, sauces, and toppings.

That means it is not enough to search for:

“Hook & Ladder calories”

A more useful question is:

“How many calories are in a Medium Hook & Ladder?”

The same applies to the Italian, New York Steamer, Engineer, Firehouse Hero, Turkey Bacon Ranch, Smokehouse Beef & Cheddar Brisket, and other subs.

Always match the exact size before comparing calories or protein.

The Bread Is Part of the Nutrition

Firehouse Subs is known for hot sandwiches served on toasted sub rolls.

Bread contributes a meaningful amount of the meal's carbohydrates, calories, and sodium, particularly as the sandwich gets larger.

If a different bread option is available at your location, it should be treated as a real customization rather than assuming every bread choice has the same nutrition.

A sub served as a salad or another bread-free preparation should also use its own nutrition record instead of simply subtracting an estimated roll.

Hook & Ladder Is More Than Turkey and Ham

The Hook & Ladder is one of Firehouse Subs' best-known sandwiches.

It combines smoked turkey breast and Virginia honey ham with Monterey Jack cheese and standard toppings.

Those ingredients contribute protein, but the complete sandwich also includes bread, cheese, condiments, vegetables, and seasonings.

That is why the nutrition of the finished Hook & Ladder is much more useful than looking only at the turkey and ham.

If you remove mayo, cheese, or another ingredient, your customized total may differ from the standard recipe.

Meat and Cheese Are Steamed Together

One feature that distinguishes Firehouse Subs from many sandwich chains is the way meats and cheeses are heated before being placed on the toasted roll.

Nutrition does not change simply because an ingredient is steamed, but it does help explain why the standard sandwich should be treated as a defined recipe rather than a pile of interchangeable deli ingredients.

A New York Steamer, Italian, Engineer, and Firehouse Hero all use different combinations of meats, cheeses, sauces, and toppings.

Choose the actual sandwich recipe whenever possible.

Protein Can Be High, but Compare the Whole Sandwich

Many Firehouse Subs sandwiches provide substantial protein.

The newer Chicken ’N’ Cheddar Rancher , for example, uses grilled white-meat chicken, bacon, cheddar, and buttermilk ranch. Firehouse Subs has highlighted the Medium version as providing around 49g of protein.

That is useful information, but protein should not be considered alone.

Chicken, bacon, cheese, ranch, bread, and other ingredients also contribute calories, fat, carbohydrates, and sodium.

A better comparison is:

Protein + calories + fat + carbs + sodium

rather than simply choosing whichever sandwich has the highest protein number.

Brisket Subs Have a Different Macro Profile

The Smokehouse Beef & Cheddar Brisket is not nutritionally comparable to a lean turkey sub.

It combines smoked beef brisket with cheddar and BBQ-style components, creating a richer sandwich with a different balance of protein, fat, carbohydrates, and sugar.

This is another reason a general “Firehouse sub calories” estimate is not very useful.

The exact sandwich matters just as much as the size.

Mayo, Ranch, BBQ Sauce, and Other Extras Count

Condiments can be easy to overlook because they appear inside the sandwich rather than beside it.

Mayo, ranch, BBQ sauce, honey-chipotle sauce, mustard, and other dressings have their own nutrition.

Removing mayo may change a sandwich noticeably, while adding extra cheese, bacon, or sauce can move the total in the opposite direction.

When NutritionSolver provides ingredient-level customization, include only what is actually on your sandwich.

New Menu Items Need Their Own Nutrition

Firehouse Subs continues to update its menu.

In 2026 the brand introduced the Chicken ’N’ Cheddar Rancher as a permanent menu item and also rolled out an improved Steak & Cheese Melt.

More recently, Smokin’ Honey Melts added grilled chicken or steak with peppers, onions, provolone, mayo, and honey-chipotle dressing.

These sandwiches should have their own nutrition records.

Do not estimate a Smokin’ Honey Chicken Melt from a different chicken sandwich or treat the current Steak & Cheese Melt as identical to an older steak sub recipe.

A Combo Is More Than the Sub

If you turn your sandwich into a meal, the side and beverage belong in the total.

A Firehouse Subs order might include:

Medium sub + chips + drink

or

Small sub + soup + cookie

or

Large sub + fries + beverage

These can have very different calorie and macro totals even when the sandwich itself stays the same.

For the most useful result, build the entire order.

Fries and Chips Should Be Kept Separate

Firehouse Subs offers several side choices depending on location and current menu availability.

Chips and fries are not nutritionally interchangeable.

They have different serving weights, preparation methods, calorie totals, fat, carbohydrates, and sodium.

If your selected meal includes chips, do not substitute fry nutrition simply because both are potato sides.

Use the exact side and size.

Soup Can Change the Meal in a Different Way

Soup may provide fewer calories than some fried sides, but it can contribute significant sodium.

That makes it useful to compare more than calories when choosing between soup, chips, fries, salad, or another side.

For a meal-level comparison, check:

Calories

Protein

Carbohydrates

Fat

Sodium

after every component has been added.

Salads Need Dressing Included Correctly

Firehouse Subs also offers salads and salad-style meals.

A salad made with turkey, chicken, ham, cheese, or another protein should use its own nutrition instead of being treated as a sandwich without bread.

Dressing matters too.

If dressing is already included in a complete salad nutrition value, do not add it twice. If the published value excludes dressing, add the serving you actually use.

Cookies and Brownies Are Separate Menu Items

Desserts can add several hundred calories after the sandwich and side have already been counted.

A cookie, brownie, or other dessert should always be added separately to your order.

For someone comparing complete meals, this gives a clearer result than treating dessert as an insignificant extra.
