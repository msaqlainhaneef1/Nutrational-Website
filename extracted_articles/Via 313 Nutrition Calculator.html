Via 313 Nutrition Calculator

Via 313 is built around Detroit-style pizza, and that changes how I would calculate a meal compared with a typical thin-crust pizza restaurant.

A Detroit-style slice has a thick, airy crust, cheese that reaches the edges, and substantial toppings. A single square can therefore contain more calories, fat, protein, and sodium than its size might suggest.

The Via 313 Nutrition Calculator helps you look beyond the size of the slice. Use it to compare pizzas, calculate several slices, add appetizers or salads, and estimate the nutrition of your complete meal.

You can review calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, sodium, saturated fat, fiber, and other available nutrition values before deciding what fits your meal.

Why Detroit-Style Pizza Needs a Per-Slice Calculator

Pizza nutrition can be difficult to estimate by sight.

A large thin slice can sometimes contain fewer calories than a smaller-looking square of Detroit-style pizza because the thickness, cheese, crust, meat, and toppings differ.

That makes the serving definition important.

Via 313 publishes pizza nutrition on a per-slice basis, so the easiest way to estimate your meal is:

Nutrition per slice × number of slices eaten

If one slice contains 330 calories and you eat two:

330 × 2 = 660 calories

If you eat three:

330 × 3 = 990 calories

The same calculation applies to protein, carbohydrates, fat, and sodium.

This is much more useful than trying to estimate the calories from the physical size of your plate.

One Slice Can Be a Substantial Portion

A Detroit-style pizza is not the type of pizza where I would automatically assume that one slice is a small amount of food.

The thick crust and edge-to-edge cheese make each square relatively dense.

Depending on the recipe and toppings, one slice can provide several hundred calories before you add:

  • Another slice
  • Cheese bread
  • Meatballs
  • Salad dressing
  • Soda
  • Dessert
  • Dipping sauces

That does not mean you need to stop at one slice.

It simply means counting the number of slices gives you a much more realistic estimate of your meal.

Detroit-Style vs Bar-Style Pizza

Via 313 currently serves both Detroit-style and bar-style pizza.

They should not be treated as nutritionally interchangeable.

Detroit-style pizza has a thicker crust and characteristic cheesy edges.

Bar-style pizza is thinner, with a crisp and chewy crust.

Even when the topping combination has a similar name, the different crust and preparation can change calories, carbohydrates, fat, and other nutrition values.

If you are choosing between the two styles, make sure you select the exact version you order.

Do not use the Detroit-style nutrition value for a bar-style slice or vice versa.

Start With the Pizza Style

A useful way to calculate your meal is to make decisions in this order:

First, choose between Detroit-style and bar-style pizza.

Second, choose the pizza recipe.

Third, decide how many slices you expect to eat.

Fourth, add appetizers, salads, drinks, sauces, or desserts.

This approach prevents one of the most common pizza-tracking mistakes: entering one slice when you actually ate several.

What About Gluten-Free Crust?

Via 313 currently offers a gluten-free vegan crust option for Detroit-style pizzas.

Gluten-free does not automatically mean lower calorie.

A gluten-free crust can still contain substantial carbohydrates and calories.

People sometimes choose gluten-free food expecting it to be lighter, but gluten content and calorie content are separate issues.

Choose the gluten-free version when it fits your dietary needs or preference, then check its nutrition separately.

Gluten-Free Is Especially Important for Allergy Awareness

Via 313 offers gluten-free options, but people with severe allergies or medically necessary gluten avoidance should pay attention to restaurant preparation practices.

A restaurant kitchen can handle wheat-containing dough alongside gluten-free products.

For someone choosing gluten-free as a preference, that may not be a concern.

For someone with celiac disease or a severe allergy, cross-contact can be much more important than the calorie number.

Nutrition information and allergen safety answer different questions.

Calculate Two Slices Before You Order

Two slices is a common pizza meal, so it helps to look at the doubled numbers before ordering.

36 grams fat
1,500 milligrams sodium

Nutrition Information Disclaimer

Via 313 nutrition values should be treated as estimates based on published restaurant information and standard menu preparation.

Actual nutrition may vary because of portion size, ingredient quantities, substitutions, preparation methods, regional menu differences, recipe updates, toppings, sauces, and other customizations.

Pizza nutrition is especially sensitive to portion size. Make sure you multiply per-slice values by the number of slices you actually consume.

Gluten-free, vegan, vegetarian, and dairy-free menu labels should not be treated as guarantees against allergen cross-contact. Anyone with celiac disease, severe food allergies, or medically necessary dietary restrictions should confirm current preparation and allergen information directly with the restaurant.

Use the calculator as a practical tool for comparing menu choices and estimating your meal rather than as an exact measurement or substitute for individualized medical nutrition advice.
