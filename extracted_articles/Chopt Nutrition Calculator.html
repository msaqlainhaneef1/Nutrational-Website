Chopt Nutrition Calculator

A salad can look simple until you count everything that goes into it.

At Chopt, the greens are only the starting point. Protein, grains, cheese, avocado, nuts, croutons, roasted vegetables, and especially dressing can all change the calories and macros of the finished meal.

Use the Chopt Nutrition Calculator above to compare salads and bowls, check individual ingredients, and see how different choices affect your complete order.

Start With the Salad You Actually Order

Chopt offers complete recipes such as the Classic Cobb, Kale Caesar, Mexican Caesar, Santa Fe, and other signature salads.

If you order one exactly as listed, the nutrition for the complete salad is the most useful number.

But once you start removing ingredients, adding protein, swapping dressing, or adding extra toppings, your meal begins to differ from the standard recipe.

That is where individual ingredient nutrition becomes more useful.

Instead of thinking only:

“How many calories are in a Chopt salad?”

think:

Greens + protein + choppings + dressing = your actual salad

The Greens Usually Are Not the Biggest Calorie Decision

Romaine, spinach, kale, arugula, and other greens provide the base of many Chopt meals, but the ingredients added afterward often make a much larger difference to the final calorie total.

For example, cheese, avocado, nuts, grains, crispy toppings, roasted ingredients, and proteins can each add considerably more than a simple vegetable topping.

This means switching lettuce varieties may change the nutrition slightly, while changing several toppings or the dressing can alter the meal much more.

When customizing a salad, focus on the full ingredient combination instead of judging the meal by its greens alone.

Dressing Can Change the Salad Quickly

Chopt is especially known for its housemade dressings, and dressing deserves its own place in the calculation.

Different dressings can have very different amounts of calories, fat, carbohydrates, and sodium.

For example, the current NutritionSolver database lists:

Mexican Caesar Dressing: 170 calories

Balsamic Vinaigrette: 140 calories

Buttermilk Ranch: 140 calories

Mexican Goddess Dressing: 90 calories

The difference becomes even more important if you use additional dressing.

If the salad nutrition already includes its standard dressing, avoid adding that same serving again. If you change the dressing, calculate the version that most closely matches your order.

Protein Can Turn a Salad Into a Very Different Meal

Chicken, steak, shrimp, salmon, falafel, tofu, and other protein options do more than increase the protein number.

They can also change calories, total fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, and sodium.

The current Chopt data shows how different complete salads can be:

Classic Cobb Salad: 600 calories and 60g protein

Southwest Steak Salad: 445 calories and 32g protein

Crispy Chicken Ranch Salad: 575 calories and 44g protein

Shrimp Spring Roll Salad: 260 calories and 18g protein

These meals differ because of the entire recipe, not just the protein.

A useful comparison therefore looks at protein together with calories, carbs, fat, fiber, and sodium .

Warm Bowls Need a Different Comparison From Salads

Chopt also offers bowls built with ingredients such as cauliflower rice, grains, proteins, vegetables, hummus, and sauces.

These should not automatically be compared with salads as if the base were the only difference.

For example, the current database lists:

Chicken Tinga Bowl with Cauliflower Rice: 515 calories

Salmon Avocado Bowl with Cauliflower Rice: 460 calories

Mediterranean Hummus Bowl with Cauliflower Rice: 535 calories

Even when bowls use the same general base, protein, hummus, avocado, sauces, and other ingredients can produce very different macro totals.

Compare the finished bowl rather than assuming every cauliflower-rice meal has similar nutrition.

Fiber Gives Useful Context Beyond Carbs

Salads and bowls can contain a meaningful amount of carbohydrate from grains, fruit, beans, corn, sweet potatoes, croutons, and other toppings.

But total carbohydrates do not tell the whole story.

Many Chopt meals also contain substantial fiber from vegetables, greens, legumes, seeds, and grains.

For example, the Southwest Steak Salad currently shows 54g of total carbohydrates with 15g of fiber .

Looking at carbohydrates and fiber together gives more useful context than treating every gram of carbohydrate as nutritionally identical.

Sodium Can Vary Widely Between Salads

A salad is not automatically a low-sodium meal.

Cheese, seasoned proteins, dressings, olives, pickled vegetables, roasted toppings, and other ingredients can raise sodium quickly.

The current database illustrates the difference:

Sweet Apple Orchard Salad: 136mg sodium

Mexican Caesar Salad: 475mg sodium

The Chopt Greek Salad: 1,070mg sodium

Classic Cobb Salad: 1,838mg sodium

These examples show why two meals from the same salad restaurant can have very different sodium profiles.

If sodium is important to your comparison, check the total after the protein, toppings, and dressing are all included.

Seasonal Salads Should Be Checked Separately

Chopt regularly rotates seasonal ingredients and recipes.

Current summer offerings include the Summer Corn Caesar Salad and Sweet Peach Burrata Bowl , which use ingredients and dressings that are different from the core menu.

NutritionSolver currently lists the Summer Corn Caesar Salad at 455 calories and the Sweet Peach Burrata Bowl at 520 calories .

Seasonal items should always use their own nutrition information rather than being estimated from a similar permanent-menu salad.

Build a Custom Chopt Salad Carefully

If you create your own salad, calculate it in layers:

Choose the greens

Then add:

Protein

Vegetables and other choppings

Cheese, nuts, grains, or crunchy toppings

Dressing

If you request extra protein, double dressing, avocado, or additional premium toppings, include those additional portions where the data is available.

This ingredient-level approach is much more useful than trying to apply one average calorie figure to every custom salad.
