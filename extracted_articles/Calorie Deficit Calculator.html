Plan Your Calorie Deficit

A calorie deficit happens when you consume fewer calories than your body uses over time. That difference creates the energy gap needed for weight loss.

The Calorie Deficit Calculator above starts with your estimated maintenance calories, or TDEE, and subtracts your chosen daily deficit. It then shows your estimated calorie target and a simple projection of how long different weight-loss milestones may take.

If you do not know your maintenance calories yet, calculate your TDEE first before using this tool.

How Your Calorie Target Is Calculated

The basic calculation is straightforward:

Daily calorie target = TDEE - daily calorie deficit

For example, if your estimated TDEE is 2,400 calories and you choose a 500-calorie daily deficit , your estimated daily target would be:

2,400 - 500 = 1,900 calories per day

This number is a planning estimate, not a guarantee that your body weight will change at exactly the same rate every week.

Why the Weight Loss Timeline Is an Estimate

Calorie math can give you a useful starting point, but real-world weight loss is rarely perfectly linear.

As body weight changes, energy needs can also change. Day-to-day scale weight is influenced by water, stored carbohydrate, food in the digestive system, sodium intake, and other normal fluctuations.

This means a calculated timeline is best viewed as a projection rather than a deadline . Two people following the same estimated calorie deficit may not lose weight at exactly the same pace.

Looking at your weight trend over time usually tells you more than reacting to a single weigh-in.

Does a Larger Calorie Deficit Mean Faster Weight Loss?

On paper, a larger deficit produces a faster projected rate of weight loss. In practice, the biggest deficit is not automatically the best choice.

A calorie target still needs to provide enough food and nutrients to support normal daily needs. A plan that feels overly restrictive can also be difficult to maintain consistently.

For many people, gradual and steady weight loss is easier to sustain than trying to lose weight as quickly as possible.

When Should You Recalculate Your Deficit?

Your original calorie target may become less accurate as your body weight, activity level, or routine changes.

Consider recalculating your TDEE and calorie deficit after a meaningful change in body weight or activity, rather than assuming your starting calorie target will remain accurate indefinitely.

Small day-to-day changes on the scale do not necessarily mean your calorie target needs to be adjusted.
