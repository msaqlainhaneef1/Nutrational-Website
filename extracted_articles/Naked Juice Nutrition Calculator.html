Naked Juice Nutrition Calculator

Naked smoothies can look similar on the shelf while having very different nutrition.

A fruit-forward Machine smoothie may contain more than 200 calories and around 50 grams of total sugar in one bottle. A Protein smoothie can provide 20 grams of plant-based protein with considerably less sugar, while the newer Fiber smoothies contain 9 grams of fiber per 15.2-ounce bottle.

Use the Naked Juice Nutrition Calculator above to compare the exact bottle you are drinking, including calories, carbohydrates, total sugar, fiber, protein, fat, sodium, and other available nutrients.

The Bottle Is the Most Useful Serving

For most current single-serve Naked smoothies, the clearest comparison is:

1 bottle = 15.2 fl oz

That matters because people commonly drink the whole bottle rather than measuring a smaller glass.

Whenever NutritionSolver has nutrition for a 15.2-ounce bottle, it should display the full-bottle value prominently.

Larger multi-serve bottles are different. Those should show both:

Nutrition per serving

and

Nutrition for the entire container

so an 8-ounce serving is not confused with a 46- or 64-ounce bottle.

Naked Has Changed Its Smoothies Recently

Current Naked smoothies are not necessarily nutritionally identical to bottles sold several years ago.

The company refreshed its lineup in late 2025. Its six Machine smoothies are now boosted with fiber and several vitamins, while its Protein and Fiber ranges were reformulated around more specific functional nutrition.

This means an older online value for Green Machine or Blue Machine may no longer describe the bottle currently sold in stores.

For this brand, the formula date matters almost as much as the flavor name .

Total Sugar Is Not the Same as Added Sugar

This distinction is especially important on Naked pages.

Current Naked smoothies are marketed with 0g added sugar .

That does not mean they contain zero total sugar.

For example, Green Machine currently contains about 49g total sugar in one bottle even though its Nutrition Facts label reports 0g added sugar .

Those two values answer different questions:

Total sugar includes sugars naturally present in the fruit and juice ingredients.

Added sugar is the amount classified as added sugars on the Nutrition Facts label.

NutritionSolver should always keep these fields separate.

“No Added Sugar” Does Not Mean Low Calorie

A smoothie can contain no added sugar and still provide a significant amount of energy.

Fruit juice, fruit purée, banana, mango, grapes, pineapple, and other fruit ingredients naturally contain carbohydrates and sugar.

That means a bottle can legitimately contain:

0g added sugar

while still containing:

200+ calories and 40g+ total sugar

There is no contradiction between those numbers.

The ingredient profile explains more than the product color.

That makes the formula version critical when comparing a current bottle with older nutrition databases.

Coconut Water Is Not a Smoothie

Although it appears within the Naked product family, coconut water belongs to a separate beverage category.

It does not contain the fruit-purée structure or protein/fiber formulation of the smoothie lines.

Large Bottles Need Serving Math

Several Machine flavors are also sold in larger bottles such as 46 or 64 ounces.

The label on a large bottle may show nutrition for an 8-ounce serving .

If you pour 16 ounces, you consumed two published servings.

If you calculate the whole bottle, multiply by the total servings per container.

NutritionSolver should never place an 8-ounce large-bottle value beside a 15.2-ounce single-bottle value without showing the serving size.
