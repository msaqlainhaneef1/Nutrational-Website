Dave’s Hot Chicken Nutrition Calculator

Dave’s Hot Chicken has a relatively focused menu, but calculating an order is not as simple as counting pieces of chicken.

A tender can come with bread, pickles, and Dave’s Sauce. A slider adds a potato bun, kale slaw, pickles, and sauce. Combo meals add fries, while newer menu items combine chicken, cheese, sauces, fries, or mac and cheese in the same order.

Use the Dave’s Hot Chicken Nutrition Calculator above to choose the exact menu item and see the available calories, protein, carbs, fat, sodium, and complete meal total.

Start With a Tender or Slider

The easiest way to understand Dave’s menu is to separate its two signature items.

A Tender is a large piece of breaded chicken served with sliced bread, pickles, and Dave’s Sauce when ordered as the standard single-tender item.

A Slider puts the chicken on a potato bun with kale slaw, pickles, and Dave’s Sauce.

Because the slider includes a bun and additional ingredients, it should not be calculated as if it were simply a tender with a different name.

If you order the chicken without some of the standard accompaniments, the nutrition you actually consume may differ from the published value for the complete menu item.

Spice Level Can Affect More Than Heat

Dave’s offers multiple heat levels, from No Spice and Lite Mild through Mild, Medium, Hot, Extra Hot, and Reaper.

The nutrition guide distinguishes lower-spice preparations from the more heavily seasoned versions, so two otherwise identical tenders or sliders can have somewhat different calorie, fat, and sodium totals depending on the preparation.

For nutrition tracking, the most useful entry is therefore:

Menu item + heat level

rather than the menu item alone.

This is a particularly important feature for a Dave’s nutrition calculator because many restaurant calculators ignore spice-level differences.

Know What Your Number Includes

This is one of the most important details on the entire Dave’s Hot Chicken page.

A published calorie value for a Single Tender may represent the standard served item, including its accompaniments, rather than only the isolated piece of chicken.

Likewise, a combo such as Dave’s #1, #2, #3, or #4 represents a different serving structure from an individual tender or slider.

Before adding bread, pickles, sauce, or fries separately, check whether they are already included in that menu item’s nutrition record.

Otherwise, you can accidentally count the same food twice.

Dave’s #1, #2, #3, and #4 Are Different Meals

The core meal combinations are built around different quantities of tenders and sliders.

Dave’s #1: 2 Tenders with Fries

Dave’s #2: 2 Sliders with Fries

Dave’s #3: 1 Tender and 1 Slider with Fries

Dave’s #4: 1 Slider with Fries

Because a slider includes a bun, kale slaw, pickles, and sauce, the #2 meal is not nutritionally equivalent to replacing its sliders with tenders.

Use the exact numbered meal when you order the standard combo. If you change the sides or remove components, build the altered order from individual items where possible.

Dave’s Sauce Deserves Its Own Attention

Dave’s Sauce is a significant part of the standard menu experience.

It appears directly on sliders and is also served as a dipping sauce with tenders and several meals. Extra sauce can be ordered separately.

That means there is an important difference between:

One included serving of Dave’s Sauce

and

Included sauce + one or more extra sauce portions

If the sauce is already included in the published nutrition for the entrée or combo, do not add it again unless you actually order or consume an extra portion.

The same principle applies to ranch, cheese sauce, spicy buffalo, spicy BBQ, and other dipping options.

Fries Can Turn an Individual Item Into a Much Larger Meal

Dave’s crinkle-cut fries are included with the main numbered meals but are also sold separately.

This distinction matters because an individual slider and a slider-with-fries meal are different nutrition entries.

Cheese Fries and Top-Loaded Fries add more ingredients on top of the fries, so they should also be treated as separate menu items instead of variations with the same nutrition.

For an accurate total, match the exact fry preparation and serving size.

Dave’s Bites Need the Correct Serving

Dave’s Bites provide another way to order the chicken without using a full tender or slider format.

The current menu includes individual Dave’s Bites meals as well as larger Hot Box servings intended for groups.

Do not use the nutrition for a catering box as if it were a personal serving.

If you are sharing a larger order, calculate the portion you actually eat.

Cauliflower Is Not a Zero-Calorie Substitute

Dave’s NOT Chicken (Cauli)™ options replace the chicken with breaded cauliflower, but that does not make the finished item calorie-free or automatically much lighter.

A Cauli Tender or Cauli Slider can still include breading, frying oil, buns, sauce, slaw, fries, or other components depending on the order.

Treat each cauliflower menu item as its own recipe rather than subtracting chicken from the standard version.

The 2026 Menu Goes Beyond Tenders and Sliders

Dave’s current menu has expanded beyond its original core combinations.

Depending on location, newer choices can include Mini Sliders, Hot Mozz, Dave’s Big Trio, and Top-Loaded Spicy Buffalo Mac , alongside existing Bites, cauliflower items, fries, shakes, and other sides.

These items combine several ingredients, so their nutrition should come from their own current recipe instead of being estimated from an older slider or tender.

Seasonal and newer items should also carry a reviewed or updated date so users know how current the information is.

Shakes Can Add a Separate Meal-Sized Amount

Classic and Top-Loaded Shakes should be calculated separately from the savory order.

Chocolate, vanilla, strawberry, and topping-heavy shakes can contain substantial calories, carbohydrates, sugar, and fat.

A meal calculation should therefore follow the entire order:

Tender or slider meal + side or extra sauce + drink or shake

rather than stopping after the chicken.

Calculate the Order Without Double-Counting

Dave’s Hot Chicken is a good example of why serving definitions matter.

The nutrition for the chicken alone, a standard single tender, a slider, and a full numbered meal can all represent different combinations of food.

Match your item, heat level, sides, sauces, and drinks to what you actually order. This gives you a much more useful calorie and macro total than combining menu numbers without checking what each one includes.
