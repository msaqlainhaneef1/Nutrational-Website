Understanding Your Ideal Weight Estimate

There is no single body weight that is “perfect” for everyone. An ideal weight calculator provides a reference estimate based mainly on your height and sex, using formulas developed for clinical use.

The calculator above gives you results from four established methods: Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi . Instead of relying on one formula, you can compare several estimates and see how they relate to a broader healthy weight range.

Think of the result as a useful reference point, not a number you are required to reach.

Why Does the Calculator Show Different Ideal Weights?

Each formula approaches ideal body weight slightly differently.

The Hamwi formula dates to 1964. The Devine formula , introduced in 1974, became widely used in clinical settings, particularly for estimating body weight for medication dosing. Robinson and Miller introduced alternative equations in 1983.

Because these methods use different starting weights and adjustments for height, they naturally produce slightly different answers.

That variation is useful. It shows why ideal weight is better viewed as an estimated range rather than one exact number.

How Body Frame Size Affects Your Result

Two people can be the same height but have different natural builds. This calculator lets you select a small, medium, or large body frame to add more context to the formula estimates.

Frame size does not determine whether a particular weight is healthy, and it cannot measure muscle or body fat. It simply helps illustrate why one fixed ideal weight may not suit every person of the same height.

Ideal Weight Is Not the Same as a Goal Weight

Your calculated ideal weight should not automatically become your target weight.

Muscle mass, body composition, age, health history, and individual build can all affect what weight is appropriate for you. Someone who carries more muscle, for example, may naturally weigh more than a traditional ideal weight formula suggests.

This is also why the calculator shows a healthy BMI weight range alongside the formula estimates. The range provides additional context rather than forcing your result into one exact target.
