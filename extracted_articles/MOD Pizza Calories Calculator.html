MOD Pizza Calories Calculator

MOD Pizza works differently from a traditional pizza menu.

You can choose a signature pizza or start from scratch, select your size and crust, choose a sauce and cheese, add as many toppings as you want, and finish the pizza with another sauce or seasoning. Because the price does not increase with every standard topping, two pizzas that cost the same can have very different calories and macros.

Use the MOD Pizza Calories Calculator above to compare signature pizzas or calculate the nutrition of the exact pizza, salad, toppings, and dessert you order.

MOD Pizza Nutrition Starts With the Size

MOD currently uses three main pizza formats:

Mini: 6-inch pizza

MOD: 11-inch pizza

Mega: 11-inch pizza with a thicker crust

These are not simply Small, Medium, and Large versions of the exact same base.

The Mega uses a thicker crust, while the Mini uses a much smaller 6-inch crust. That means the size selection changes the nutrition before any sauce, cheese, meat, or vegetables are added.

For a custom pizza, always choose the size first.

Mega Is About Crust Thickness, Not a Wider Pizza

The name Mega can be misleading if you expect a much wider pizza.

MOD describes both its standard MOD pizza and Mega as 11 inches. The difference is that Mega uses a much thicker crust.

MOD's July 2026 nutrition information lists approximately:

Mini 6-inch crust: 200 calories

MOD 11-inch thin crust: 467 calories

Mega 11-inch thick crust: 933 calories

That means switching from MOD to Mega can make a major difference before the toppings change at all.

Unlimited Toppings Make Ingredient-Level Calculation Especially Useful

MOD's defining feature is the ability to customize from more than 40 toppings for one price.

From a nutrition perspective, however, unlimited toppings do not mean unlimited toppings have the same effect.

Adding:

basil + mushrooms + spinach

is very different from adding:

sausage + ground beef + bacon + extra cheese

even if MOD charges the same menu price.

This makes a real nutrition calculator much more useful than a fixed list of pizzas.

A custom MOD pizza should be calculated in this order:

Size and crust

Sauce

Cheese

Meat or plant-based protein

Vegetables and other toppings

Finishing sauce

Sauce Can Be Almost Negligible or a Major Addition

MOD's sauce choices vary significantly.

The current menu lists approximately:

Tomato Sauce: 5 calories per tablespoon

Spicy Tomato Sauce: 12 calories

Creamy Alfredo Sauce: 15 calories

Sriracha: 25 calories

Extra Virgin Olive Oil: 120 calories

The difference is substantial.

A pizza with a traditional tomato sauce starts very differently from one using a larger amount of oil-based sauce.

Sauce should therefore be a visible calculator selection, not hidden inside a generic build-your-own pizza.

Finishing Sauce Comes After the Pizza Is Built

MOD also lets customers finish pizzas with sauces and seasonings after the main toppings are added.

This is an important difference from pizza chains where the sauce appears only underneath the cheese.

A finishing sauce can contribute additional calories, carbs, sugar, fat, or sodium.

For the most accurate estimate, calculate:

Pizza recipe + finishing sauce

rather than stopping when the toppings are added.

Gluten-Friendly and Cauliflower Crust Need Separate Nutrition

MOD currently offers both a gluten-friendly crust and a cauliflower crust in addition to its standard crusts.

The July 2026 official nutrition information lists approximately:

Gluten-Friendly Crust: 650 calories

Cauliflower Crust: 520 calories

These should be treated as separate pizza bases.

Do not assume a cauliflower crust is automatically the lowest-calorie MOD option, and do not calculate the gluten-friendly crust by simply removing wheat nutrition from the regular pizza.

The complete crust recipe determines the value.

MOD also states that all pizzas are prepared on a shared serving line, so none of its crust options are recommended for people with gluten sensitivity based solely on the gluten-friendly name.

Mini Salad and MOD Salad Are Different Serving Types

MOD uses different salad sizes for different purposes.

The Mini is positioned as a side, while the MOD size is an entrée.

For example:

Mini Caesar: about 223 calories

MOD Caesar: about 446 calories

Mini Garden: about 109 calories

MOD Garden: about 219 calories

The portion label should always appear in the item name so a side salad is not confused with a complete entrée salad.

A Mini Pizza and Mini Salad Can Form a Lunch Combo

Some current locations offer a MOD Lunch Combo combining an unlimited-topping Mini Pizza with a Mini Salad.

That creates another useful meal-builder scenario.

Instead of showing one average Lunch Combo number, the calculator should let the user select:

Mini pizza recipe

plus

Mini salad recipe

because the total can vary considerably depending on both choices.

Build the MOD Order You Actually Create

MOD nutrition makes the most sense when the calculator follows the same process as the restaurant.

Start with the Mini, MOD, or Mega size and choose the exact crust .

Then add the sauce, cheese, proteins, vegetables, other toppings, and finishing sauce .

For salads, build the entrée from the greens and toppings you select.

Finally, add any Kids Meal components, drink, No Name Cake, or Mega Cookie that is part of the order.

That creates a much more useful MOD Pizza nutrition result than relying on one generic calorie number for a pizza.
