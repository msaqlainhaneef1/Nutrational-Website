Find Your Estimated Daily Calorie Needs

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure, or TDEE , is an estimate of how many calories your body uses across a typical day.

Unlike BMR, which focuses on energy used at rest, TDEE accounts for your overall activity level. That makes it a useful estimate of the calories you may need to maintain your current body weight.

Enter your age, sex, height, weight, and usual activity level in the TDEE Calculator above to get your estimated daily maintenance calories.

Where Your TDEE Number Comes From

NutritionSolver calculates your TDEE in two steps.

First, it estimates your resting energy needs using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation . This gives an estimate of the energy your body uses before normal daily activity is considered.

Your estimated resting needs are then adjusted using the activity level you select.

In simple terms:

TDEE = estimated resting energy needs × activity factor

This produces an estimate that reflects both your body size and how active you are during a typical week.

Choose an Activity Level That Matches Real Life

Activity level can make a noticeable difference to your TDEE result, so choose the option that best represents your normal routine rather than your most active day.

Someone who works at a desk but exercises several times per week may still have lower daily energy expenditure than someone with an active job who spends much of the day walking or lifting.

Your workouts matter, but so does everything you do outside the gym.

Walking, standing, commuting, household tasks, physical work, and other everyday movement can all contribute to your total energy expenditure.

If you are between two activity levels, the calculator should be treated as a starting estimate rather than an exact measurement.

What Does Your TDEE Result Tell You?

If your result is 2,300 calories per day , the calculator estimates that your body uses roughly 2,300 calories during an average day at the activity level you selected.

That number is commonly called your maintenance calorie estimate .

It does not mean your body will burn exactly 2,300 calories every day. Energy expenditure naturally changes with activity, body weight, training, and normal differences between days.

The most useful way to think about TDEE is as a practical baseline for understanding your overall energy needs.

If your main goal is to calculate a specific calorie reduction after finding your maintenance estimate, use the Calorie Deficit Calculator rather than trying to turn the TDEE result itself into a weight-loss prescription.

Why Your Actual TDEE May Be Different

Every TDEE calculator relies on prediction equations and activity estimates.

Your real energy expenditure can differ because of factors such as:

  • body composition
  • changes in daily movement
  • exercise volume
  • occupation
  • differences in resting energy expenditure

Activity level is especially difficult to estimate perfectly. Two people with similar height, weight, and workout schedules can still move very different amounts during the rest of the day.

For that reason, your calculated TDEE should be viewed as an estimate, not an exact calorie measurement .

When Should You Calculate TDEE Again?

Your energy needs are not permanently fixed.

It can make sense to recalculate your TDEE after a meaningful change in body weight, activity level, training routine, or daily lifestyle.

There is usually no need to recalculate because of small day-to-day changes. TDEE is more useful for understanding your general energy needs over time.
