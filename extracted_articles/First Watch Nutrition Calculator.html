First Watch Nutrition Calculator

First Watch serves breakfast and brunch plates that often contain several foods in one menu item.

An omelet may come with toast and potatoes. Oatmeal can include fruit, pecans, milk, brown sugar, and a muffin. Pancakes may include butter and syrup. Avocado Toast comes with eggs. A breakfast sandwich may also arrive with a side.

That makes one detail especially important when calculating First Watch nutrition: know what the listed nutrition actually includes .

Use the First Watch Nutrition Calculator above to compare complete menu items, check calories and macros, and combine sides, drinks, or other additions that are not already part of the listed meal.

Start With the Complete Plate

At First Watch, many nutrition numbers represent an entire standard restaurant plate rather than one isolated ingredient.

For example, the current Traditional Breakfast contains about:

1,000 calories
30g protein
81g carbohydrates
59g fat
2,630mg sodium

That total reflects the standard meal configuration, not just the eggs.

Before adding toast, potatoes, fruit, bacon, preserves, or another component separately, check whether it is already included in the menu item.

This helps prevent double-counting.

Why Steel-Cut Oatmeal Can Be Nearly 1,000 Calories

First Watch's Steel-Cut Oatmeal is a perfect example of why serving details matter.

The complete adult menu item is currently about 995 calories , but the oatmeal itself is only one part of the plate.

The standard order also includes ingredients such as:

Banana

Berries

Pecans

Reduced-fat milk

Brown sugar

A freshly baked muffin

Those additions explain why the complete menu item has far more calories than a plain bowl of oatmeal.

If you skip the muffin, brown sugar, nuts, or another component, your actual meal will differ from the published standard recipe.

Avocado Toast Includes Eggs

First Watch Avocado Toast is another item where the name does not describe the whole plate.

The standard meal currently contains about:

580 calories

24g protein

38g carbohydrates

39g fat

14g fiber

It includes thick-cut whole grain toast with smashed avocado plus two cage-free eggs .

If you change the eggs to egg whites, poached eggs, or another preparation, the total changes.

Do not add another two eggs to the standard Avocado Toast entry unless you actually order extra eggs.

Omelet Calories Include More Than Eggs

First Watch omelets combine eggs with cheese, meats, avocado, vegetables, and other fillings, and they are served as complete breakfast meals.

Current examples include:

Bacado Omelet: about 1,140 calories and 45g protein

Chile Chorizo Omelet: about 1,130 calories and 45g protein

The Works Omelet: about 1,120 calories and 48g protein

These totals can look high if you think only about the eggs.

The full meal and accompaniments are what matter.

If you substitute fruit or greens for potatoes, use egg whites, or change another side, calculate the customized version when possible.

A Lower-Calorie Breakfast Can Still Provide Substantial Protein

First Watch also offers meals with a different calorie-to-protein balance.

The current Tri-Athlete contains approximately:

450 calories

31g protein

67g carbohydrates

8g fat

The Power Wrap is currently around:

500 calories

37g protein

63g carbohydrates

11g fat

These items show why protein is more useful when viewed alongside total calories, rather than simply choosing whichever entrée has the largest protein number.

Hashes Can Be Full-Size Brunch Meals

First Watch hashes combine seasoned potatoes with eggs, vegetables, cheese, meat, avocado, toast, and other ingredients depending on the recipe.

Current core options include:

Farmhouse Hash: about 1,250 calories and 37g protein

Market Hash: about 1,180 calories and 33g protein

A hash therefore should not be treated like a small side of breakfast potatoes.

It is a complete entrée.

This distinction is especially useful when comparing a hash with an omelet, bowl, toast, or sandwich.

Power Bowls Are Not Automatically Low-Calorie

The word “bowl” can sound lighter than a traditional breakfast plate, but the ingredients determine the nutrition.

Current First Watch bowls include examples such as:

Pesto Chicken Power Bowl: about 650 calories and 31g protein

Power Breakfast Bowl: about 860 calories and 33g protein

Barbacoa Chilaquiles Breakfast Bowl: about 1,290 calories and 52g protein

Protein, avocado, grains, cheese, eggs, sauces, tortilla components, and other ingredients can all change the final total.

Compare the complete bowl rather than assuming every bowl fits the same nutrition range.

Pancakes, Waffles, and French Toast Need the Full Recipe

First Watch's sweeter breakfast dishes can include much more than the bread or pancake itself.

Butter, syrup, whipped toppings, fruit, nuts, custard, sauces, and seasonal additions can contribute substantial calories and carbohydrates.

The Kids Chocolate Chip Pancake, for example, is currently about 870 calories , while the Kids Half Waffle is about 520 calories .

Adult specialty pancakes and French toast can be larger still.

When comparing sweet breakfast items, use the complete recipe rather than a generic pancake or French toast estimate.

Side Substitutions Can Change the Plate

First Watch allows substitutions on many meals.

Depending on the entrée, alternatives can include:

Fresh seasonal fruit

Fresh seasoned potatoes

Lemon-dressed mixed greens

Whole grain artisan toast

Gluten-free toast

Changing the side can alter calories, carbs, fat, fiber, and sodium.

If NutritionSolver provides the components separately, remove the standard side and add the replacement rather than counting both.

Bacon Choices Are Not Interchangeable

First Watch offers several breakfast meats and sides with very different profiles.

Current examples include:

Hardwood Smoked Bacon: about 160 calories

Million Dollar Bacon: about 250 calories

Turkey Sausage: about 220 calories

Pork Sausage: about 460 calories

Million Dollar Bacon includes a sweet glaze, so it contains considerably more carbohydrates and sugar than standard bacon.

Choose the actual breakfast meat you order rather than using one general bacon or sausage value.

Juice Can Add Significant Carbohydrates and Sugar

First Watch is known for fresh juices and specialty beverages.

For example:

Kale Tonic: about 130 calories

Morning Meditation: about 140 calories

These drinks contain little or no fat but can provide a meaningful amount of carbohydrates and naturally occurring or added sugars depending on the recipe.

A brunch calculation should include the juice if you drink it with your entrée.

Coffee Drinks Need Their Additions Counted

Plain coffee and a flavored iced coffee are very different nutrition choices.

Milk, cream, sweetener, flavored syrups, cold foam, chocolate, and seasonal ingredients can turn a simple beverage into a drink containing several hundred calories.

Select the exact prepared coffee rather than using black coffee nutrition for a flavored drink.

Seasonal Brunch Items Need Current Data

First Watch rotates a seasonal menu throughout the year.

These dishes often use completely different ingredients from the permanent menu, so a seasonal hash, French toast, sandwich, juice, or shareable should have its own nutrition entry.

Do not estimate a current seasonal recipe from an older item with a similar name.

NutritionSolver should also distinguish clearly between:

Current Seasonal Menu

and

Past Seasonal Items

so users know whether a nutrition record represents something they can currently order.

Sodium Can Be High Across a Complete Breakfast

Breakfast and brunch meals can accumulate sodium from several sources at once.

Egg dishes may include cheese, bacon, sausage, ham, tortillas, bread, sauces, seasoned potatoes, and other ingredients.

For example, current published nutrition includes:

Traditional Breakfast: about 2,630mg sodium

Farmhouse Hash: about 2,890mg

Barbacoa Chilaquiles Breakfast Bowl: about 3,300mg

Calories alone therefore do not tell the whole story.

Use the calculator to compare sodium together with calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat.
