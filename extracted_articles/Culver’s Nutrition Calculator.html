Culver’s Nutrition Calculator

Culver’s menu brings together two very different kinds of food: made-to-order savory meals and Fresh Frozen Custard desserts.

That means the nutrition of a Culver’s visit can change quickly depending on your ButterBurger size, toppings, side, dipping sauce, drink, and whether you add custard afterward .

Use the Culver’s Nutrition Calculator above to compare individual items and combine everything you order into one complete calorie and macro total.

ButterBurger Patty Count Changes the Meal Quickly

Culver’s ButterBurgers are available with different numbers of beef patties, so it is important to select the exact version you order.

The current Culver’s Deluxe, for example, is approximately:

Single: 580 calories

Double: 820 calories

Triple: 1,060 calories

Adding another patty increases protein, but it also raises total calories, fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, and usually sodium.

If you are comparing burgers, use the exact single, double, or triple entry instead of treating every ButterBurger as nutritionally similar.

Burger Toppings Can Matter More Than They Look

A Culver’s burger can also be customized with different buns, cheeses, bacon, grilled onions, mushrooms, mayo, ketchup, and other toppings.

Some changes are relatively small. Others make a much larger difference.

For example, mayo can add considerably more calories than lettuce or tomato, while bacon and extra cheese contribute additional calories, protein, fat, and sodium.

This makes the standard burger nutrition a useful starting point, but your customized order may differ.

When separate ingredients are available, calculate the burger as closely as possible to the version you actually eat.

A Value Basket Is More Than the Burger

Culver’s Value Baskets combine an entrée with a side and drink.

The burger or chicken item alone therefore does not represent the nutrition of the full meal.

Think of the basket as:

Entrée + side + beverage

The final total depends on your selections.

Choosing Crinkle Cut Fries, Wisconsin Cheese Curds, Onion Rings, soup, broccoli, or another premium side can produce very different nutrition totals.

The drink matters too. A regular soft drink adds carbohydrates and sugar, while a zero-calorie beverage changes the total differently.

Build each part of the basket when you want to understand the full order.

Wisconsin Cheese Curds Are Their Own Kind of Side

Culver’s Wisconsin Cheese Curds are not nutritionally comparable to plain cheese or a vegetable side.

They are pieces of Wisconsin cheese that are breaded and fried, so their calories come from a combination of cheese, breading, and cooking fat.

That gives them a different balance of:

Protein

Carbohydrates

Total fat

Saturated fat

Sodium

than Crinkle Cut Fries or another side.

If you switch fries for cheese curds in a Value Basket, update the side in your calculation instead of assuming the meal stays roughly the same.

Chicken Tenders Depend on Piece Count and Sauce

Culver’s Original Chicken Tenders are sold in different quantities.

The current official menu lists approximately:

1 tender: 130 calories

2 tenders: 260 calories

4 tenders: 520 calories

8 tenders: 1,040 calories

The dipping sauce is separate and can change the meal significantly.

Some sauces add only a small number of calories, while richer sauces can contribute well over 100 or even several hundred calories per serving.

For a tender meal, calculate:

Tenders + dipping sauce + side + drink

rather than looking at the chicken alone.

Grilled and Crispy Chicken Are Different Choices

Culver’s offers grilled and crispy chicken sandwiches, and the preparation changes their nutrition.

Breading adds carbohydrates and can increase fat, while grilled chicken has a different macro balance.

Both can provide substantial protein, but that does not mean their total calories, carbs, fat, or sodium are similar.

Use the complete sandwich nutrition rather than comparing them only by the amount of chicken.

Fresh Frozen Custard Needs Its Own Calculation

Culver’s Fresh Frozen Custard is a major part of the menu and should be treated separately from the savory meal.

Even a simple dish of custard contributes its own calories, carbohydrates, sugar, and fat.

Once you move into sundaes and Concrete Mixers , the number of possible combinations grows considerably.

Culver’s currently offers Concrete Mixers in several sizes:

Mini: about 380 calories before the selected mix-ins

Small: about 630 calories

Medium: about 820 calories

Large: about 940 calories

Mix-ins such as cookie dough, candy, brownies, cheesecake pieces, chocolate syrup, or nuts add their own calories on top of the custard base.

So there is no single accurate calorie number for every Concrete Mixer.

Mix-Ins Can Change a Concrete Mixer Substantially

A Concrete Mixer is useful to think about as:

Custard base + mix-in 1 + mix-in 2 + any additional toppings

Different mix-ins contribute very different amounts.

Fruit may add fewer calories than candy, cookie dough, nuts, or cheesecake pieces, while additional mix-ins increase the total further.

If you want to estimate your actual dessert, match both the size and the mix-ins rather than using only the base Concrete Mixer value.

Sodium Can Add Up Across the Savory Meal

Calories are not the only useful number to check at Culver’s.

Burgers, chicken tenders, cheese curds, sauces, cheese, and other savory sides can each contribute meaningful amounts of sodium.

Once several are combined into a Value Basket, the final sodium amount can become considerably higher than the entrée alone.

For the clearest comparison, review sodium after you have added the burger or chicken, side, sauce, and other extras .
