Mucho Burrito Nutrition Calculator

Mucho Burrito meals are built one ingredient at a time, so there is rarely one calorie number that represents every burrito or bowl.

A Small burrito with grilled chicken, rice, beans, salsa, and vegetables is a different serving from a Mucho burrito loaded with chorizo, cheese, guacamole, sour cream, and additional sauces. A bowl removes the tortilla but can use a different base, while tacos and quesadillas start with their own serving formats.

Use the Mucho Burrito Nutrition Calculator above to build the meal you actually order and see how each ingredient changes the available calories and macros.

Think in Layers, Not Just Menu Names

The easiest way to understand Mucho Burrito nutrition is to build the meal in layers.

Start with:

Size and format

Then choose:

Tortilla or bowl base

Rice and beans

Protein

Cheese

Fresh toppings

Salsa

Sauces

Guacamole or queso

Finally, add any side, dessert, or drink you order separately.

This matches Mucho Burrito's current build-your-own approach, where customers choose the individual components of burritos, bowls, salads, tacos, and quesadillas.

Small, Regular, and Mucho Are Different Serving Sizes

Mucho Burrito currently recognizes three main burrito sizes:

Small

Regular

Mucho

The larger serving is not simply the same nutrition value with a different name.

More tortilla, rice, beans, protein, cheese, and toppings can all change the final total.

NutritionSolver should therefore preserve size-specific ingredient portions wherever Mucho Burrito provides them.

A Small burrito should never automatically inherit the ingredient quantities of a Mucho burrito.

The Tortilla Can Be a Large Part of the Burrito

Mucho Burrito currently lets customers choose between white flour and whole-wheat tortillas.

Your current NutritionSolver data lists a 10-inch white flour tortilla at approximately:

340 calories

9g protein

52g carbohydrates

10g fat

The current 10-inch whole-wheat tortilla is also listed around 340 calories in the existing database.

That means the tortilla alone can account for a meaningful part of the burrito's total calories and carbohydrates.

If you choose a bowl instead, do not simply subtract a random tortilla estimate. Use the actual bowl ingredients and portions.

A Bowl Starts With a Different Decision

Mucho Burrito's current bowl builder lets customers choose among bases such as:

Cilantro-Lime Rice

Mexican Brown Rice

Romaine Lettuce

and combinations of rice and greens.

That makes the bowl more flexible than simply being a burrito without a tortilla.

A rice-heavy bowl and a greens-based bowl can have very different carbohydrate and calorie totals before the protein or toppings are added.

For bowls, calculate:

Base → protein → beans → cheese → toppings → sauces

rather than starting from a burrito total.

Grilled and Crispy Chicken Need Separate Records

Mucho Burrito currently offers both fire-grilled chicken and Crispy Chicken Tenders.

Those are different preparations and should never share the same nutrition record.

The breading and cooking method used for crispy chicken can change calories, carbs, fat, and sodium compared with grilled chicken.

NutritionSolver should therefore make the preparation obvious in the item name.

Shiitake Carnitas Should Not Be Treated as Pork

Mucho Burrito currently describes Shiitake Carnitas as a plant-based option made from seasoned shiitake mushrooms with a carnitas-style texture.

Despite the name, it is not pork carnitas.

It needs its own nutrition record and dietary labeling.

A search for “Mucho Burrito carnitas calories” should distinguish:

Pork Carnitas

from

Shiitake Carnitas

rather than combining them.

Cheese and Plant-Based Cheese Are Separate Choices

Current Mucho Burrito ordering materials include standard cheese and plant-based cheese.

These ingredients can differ in calories, protein, fat, sodium, and allergens.

If plant-based cheese is selected, the calculator should replace the standard cheese rather than adding it on top of the regular cheese value.

Always match the serving context instead of using one guacamole number for every situation.

Current Signature Builds Need More Than a Zero-Calorie Parent Record

Mucho Burrito now promotes signature builds in addition to fully customized meals.

One current example is MUCHO's Way Burrito & Bowl , which combines Mexican brown rice, fajita vegetables, black beans, cheese, salsa, pico de gallo, house-made sauces, and a chosen protein.

These finished recipes should either have verified complete nutrition or work as preloaded ingredient configurations inside the calculator.

Cali Crunch Uses Fries Inside the Burrito

Cali Crunch has a particularly distinctive structure.

The current burrito uses Spiced Mercado Fries in place of rice and combines them with grilled chicken, fajita vegetables, black beans, cheese, pico de gallo, charred corn, crispy onions, guacamole, and Amarillo sauce.

The bowl version is different because the current official description says the bowl includes rice along with the Mercado Fries.

That means:

Cali Crunch Burrito

and

Cali Crunch Bowl

should not automatically share the same nutrition formula.

Mercado Fries Are More Than a Side

Mucho Burrito currently uses Spiced Mercado Fries both as a side and as an ingredient in Cali Crunch.

This creates two different serving contexts:

Fries ordered separately

and

Fries used inside an entrée

The calculator should use the serving amount that matches the selected context.

A full side portion should not automatically be added inside a Cali Crunch recipe unless that is the verified amount used.

Tacos Need Shell Count and Filling

Mucho Burrito's taco builder allows customers to choose protein, cheese, toppings, and sauces.

NutritionSolver currently lists:

Soft Flour Tacos, 3 count: about 270 calories for the shells

and

Hard Corn Tacos, 2 count: about 50 calories for the shells.

Those are only base values.

Protein, cheese, guacamole, salsa, sauces, and other fillings still need to be added.

The calculator should therefore work as:

Shell type and count → protein → cheese → toppings → sauce

Quesadillas Need Their Own Cheese-Forward Calculation

A quesadilla starts differently from a burrito or taco.

The tortilla and melted cheese form a major part of the base, followed by the selected protein and toppings.

NutritionSolver currently contains older named quesadilla records such as:

El Clasico: about 800 calories

Super Suiza: about 1,000 calories

but current build-your-own quesadilla options should also be available ingredient by ingredient.

Do not calculate a quesadilla by taking a burrito and removing rice and beans.

Salsa Flight Is Designed for Sharing

The current menu includes a Salsa Flight pairing tortilla chips with four house-made salsas.

A shared appetizer should clearly state whether its nutrition represents:

the whole order

or

one serving

If several people share it, the calculator can only estimate your personal intake based on the amount you actually eat.

Drinks Need Exact Product Selection

Mucho Burrito currently sells beverages such as canned and bottled soft drinks, Jarritos, iced tea, juice, water, sparkling water, and other products.

A bottled water can genuinely contain zero calories.

A regular Jarritos or sugar-sweetened soft drink cannot reasonably be assumed to contain zero calories.

Each branded drink needs the exact product and serving size before a nutrition value is assigned.

Build Your Mucho Burrito Meal in the Same Order You Build It In-Store

First choose the Small, Regular, or Mucho serving and whether you want a burrito, bowl, taco, quesadilla, or salad .

Then select the correct tortilla or base, rice, beans, protein, cheese, fresh toppings, salsa, sauces, guacamole, or queso .

Finish by adding any Mercado Fries, chips and salsa, Salsa Flight, dessert, or beverage you actually consume.

That produces a much more useful Mucho Burrito nutrition estimate than treating every customizable meal as one fixed menu item.
