St. Louis Bread Co. and Panera Bread

St. Louis Bread Co. operates under the same restaurant company and menu system as Panera Bread, with the St. Louis Bread Co. name continuing to be used in its home market.

That means the menu is built around familiar bakery-cafe categories such as sandwiches, soups, salads, mac and cheese, bagels, breakfast foods, bakery items, and drinks.

When checking nutrition, make sure the menu item and serving size match the version currently offered at your bakery-cafe because menu availability can vary by location.

Half and Whole Portions Matter

One of the biggest things to watch when comparing St. Louis Bread Co. nutrition is portion size .

Sandwiches and salads may be available as half or whole portions, while soups can come in different serving sizes. A half sandwich should not be compared directly with a full sandwich without checking the serving information first.

This becomes especially important when building a combination meal.

For example:

Half sandwich + soup

may have a very different nutrition total from:

Whole sandwich + soup + side

Use the serving description shown with each item before adding it to your meal.

Soup, Sandwich, and Salad Combinations Add Up

A bakery-cafe meal often contains several items that are nutritionally very different.

A sandwich may contribute much of the meal's protein and carbohydrates. Soup can add additional calories and significant sodium. A salad may change considerably depending on the protein and dressing included.

Instead of comparing only the headline calorie number, look at the complete combination:

Calories for overall energy
Protein for the amount provided by the meal
Carbohydrates from bread, grains, soups, bakery foods, and drinks
Fat from cheese, sauces, dressings, and other ingredients
Sodium , which can add up across sandwiches, soups, dressings, and sides

This gives you a clearer picture of the meal than evaluating each item in isolation.

Bagel Nutrition Changes With the Spread

A bagel and a bagel with cream cheese are not the same nutrition entry.

St. Louis Bread Co. offers several bagel varieties, including options such as Asiago Cheese, Blueberry, Cinnamon Crunch, Everything, Plain, and Sesame.

Cream cheese and other spreads add their own calories, fat, carbohydrates, protein, and sodium.

If you use a spread, add it separately unless the nutrition listing specifically describes the bagel and spread together.

Breakfast Can Be More Than the Sandwich

Breakfast includes more than bagels.

Egg sandwiches, croissants, frittatas, soufflés, oatmeal, and other breakfast foods can have very different nutrition profiles.

Bread choice also matters. A croissant-based breakfast sandwich, bagel sandwich, or ciabatta sandwich should not be assumed to contain the same calories or macros even if the egg or meat filling is similar.

Compare the finished menu item rather than only the main ingredient.

A Bread Side Is Still Part of the Meal

Many bakery-cafe orders include bread or another side.

If your soup or salad comes with a baguette portion, chips, or another side, that food contributes separately to the total meal nutrition unless it is already included in the listed values.

This detail can be easy to miss when you are looking only at the entrée.

The meal builder is most useful when it reflects everything you actually plan to eat.

Bakery Items and Drinks Need Their Own Calculation

Pastries, cookies, bagels, specialty coffee drinks, lemonades, and other beverages can add a meaningful amount to an order even when they are not part of the main meal.

A black coffee and a sweetened specialty drink can have completely different nutrition profiles.

Likewise, adding a pastry after lunch should be counted separately from the sandwich or soup.

For a complete total, calculate:

Main meal + side + drink + bakery item

rather than stopping at the entrée.
