In-N-Out Nutrition Calculator

In-N-Out keeps its main menu simple, but the way you order a burger can change its nutrition considerably.

A Double-Double has two beef patties and two slices of cheese. A Cheeseburger has one of each. Protein Style replaces the bun with lettuce, while Animal Style changes the preparation and toppings. You can also change the spread, onions, cheese, or number of patties.

Use the In-N-Out Nutrition Calculator above to compare burgers, fries, shakes, and popular Not-So-Secret Menu choices or combine them into the meal you actually order.

Double-Double Means Two Patties and Two Cheese Slices

The Double-Double is not simply a larger Cheeseburger.

It contains:

Two 100% beef patties

Two slices of American cheese

Lettuce

Tomato

Spread

Onion, if requested

Toasted bun

The current standard Double-Double with onion contains about 610 calories, 34g protein, 42g carbohydrates, and 34g fat .

That makes it useful to compare the finished burger rather than trying to estimate it from a single beef patty.

Protein Style Changes the Bun, Not the Burger Inside

Protein Style replaces the standard toasted bun with hand-leafed lettuce.

That creates a significant difference in calories and carbohydrates.

For example, a standard Hamburger is about 360 calories , while the current Protein Style Hamburger is approximately 210 calories .

A standard Cheeseburger is about 430 calories , while its Protein Style version is approximately 280 calories .

Protein Style still contains the beef, cheese where applicable, spread, lettuce, tomato, and other selected toppings. It should therefore be treated as a separate burger configuration rather than simply subtracting all carbohydrates from the standard burger.

Animal Style Changes the Preparation

Animal Style is one of In-N-Out's best-known customizations.

The official preparation includes a mustard-cooked beef patty, pickles, extra spread, and grilled onions , along with the burger's other selected ingredients.

That means an Animal Style burger should not automatically inherit the exact nutrition of the standard version.

Extra spread, pickles, grilled onions, and the mustard-cooked preparation change the finished recipe.

If verified Animal Style nutrition is available, use that dedicated record. If only the standard burger nutrition is officially published, the calculator should clearly label an Animal Style total as estimated rather than presenting it as an official value.

Spread Can Make a Noticeable Difference

In-N-Out publishes nutrition for some burgers both with its standard spread and with mustard and ketchup instead of spread .

This is useful because spread contributes meaningful fat and calories.

For example, the standard Double-Double is approximately 610 calories , while replacing spread with mustard and ketchup lowers the current published total.

The same pattern applies to the Hamburger and Cheeseburger.

If you make this substitution, use the correct version rather than continuing to calculate the standard spread recipe.

More Patties and Cheese Need Separate Calculations

The Not-So-Secret Menu includes larger burgers such as:

3x3 , also called Triple Triple

4x4 , also called Four by Four or Quad Quad

A 3x3 contains three beef patties and three slices of cheese.

A 4x4 contains four beef patties and four slices of cheese.

These burgers should not be treated as simply “large Double-Doubles.” Each additional patty and cheese slice changes calories, protein, fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, and sodium.

When official nutrition is unavailable for a custom configuration, derived values should be identified clearly as estimates.

Double-Meat Is Different From Double-Double

The official Not-So-Secret Menu also includes the Double-Meat .

It uses two beef patties but does not add the two cheese slices found on a Double-Double.

That gives it a different calorie, fat, sodium, and protein profile.

This is an important distinction for anyone comparing higher-protein burger options.

Flying Dutchman Removes the Bun and Produce

The Flying Dutchman is one of In-N-Out's more distinctive custom orders.

Traditionally, it consists of:

Two beef patties + two slices of cheese

without the standard bun or condiments.

That makes it very different from Protein Style.

Protein Style still wraps the burger with lettuce and includes the normal burger ingredients unless changed. The Flying Dutchman strips the order down much further.

These should therefore appear as separate calculator options.

Grilled Cheese Is Not a Cheeseburger Without Meat Nutrition

In-N-Out's Grilled Cheese uses two slices of melted American cheese with lettuce, tomato, spread, and optional onions on a toasted bun.

It does not contain the beef patty.

Use its own nutrition entry rather than starting with a Cheeseburger and guessing how much removing the meat changes the result.

French Fries Are One Standard Core Side

In-N-Out's regular French Fries currently contain approximately:

360 calories

6g protein

49g carbohydrates

15g fat

150mg sodium

The fries are made from potatoes and cooked in sunflower oil.

If you add fries to a burger, calculate them separately so you can see the difference between the entrée and the complete meal.

Animal Style Fries Need Better Serving Transparency

Animal Style Fries generally add cheese, spread, and grilled onions to the regular fries.

That makes them substantially different from plain fries.

Because In-N-Out's main official nutrition guide does not provide the same complete nutrition disclosure for every Not-So-Secret configuration, NutritionSolver should make the source of an Animal Style Fries value clear.

A useful label would be:

Estimated from standard ingredients

when the number is derived rather than directly published by In-N-Out.

This gives users useful information without implying that an estimated number is an official restaurant disclosure.

Shakes Add a Significant Separate Total

In-N-Out currently publishes nutrition for three classic shakes.

Approximately:

Vanilla Shake: 590 calories

Chocolate Shake: 610 calories

Strawberry Shake: 610 calories

These 15 fl oz shakes also contain substantial carbohydrates and sugar.

A Double-Double with fries is one meal total. A Double-Double with fries and a shake is a considerably different order.

Always include the shake when calculating what you actually consume.

Build the Combo Instead of Looking Only at the Burger

In-N-Out's standard combo structure pairs:

Burger + fries + beverage

For example, Combo #1 starts with a Double-Double, Combo #2 with a Cheeseburger, and Combo #3 with a Hamburger.

The burger nutrition does not represent the whole combo.

The final total depends on whether you choose a regular soft drink, diet drink, water, shake, or another available beverage.

NutritionSolver's meal builder is especially useful here because each component can be added separately.

Sodium Can Change With Customization

A standard Double-Double currently contains more than 1,600mg of sodium .

Cheese, spread, seasoning, pickles, sauces, and additional patties can change the total further.

Protein Style may reduce carbohydrates considerably, but it does not necessarily make the burger low in sodium.

Compare sodium separately from calories and carbs if it matters to your meal planning.

Calculate Your Exact In-N-Out Order

Begin with the exact Hamburger, Cheeseburger, Double-Double, or Not-So-Secret Menu configuration you order.

Then account for whether it is standard or Protein Style , whether you use spread or mustard and ketchup , and whether you request Animal Style, additional patties, cheese, or other changes .

Finally, add the fries, beverage, or shake you actually consume.

That produces a much more useful In-N-Out nutrition total than relying on one generic number for a burger or combo.
