Chuy’s Signature Sauces Make a Real Difference

Sauces are a central part of the Chuy’s menu, not just a small garnish.

The restaurant currently offers nine made-from-scratch sauces ranging from Tangy Tomatillo and Roasted Ranchero to Creamy Green Chile, Boom-Boom, and Hatch Green Chile.

These sauces do not all have the same nutrition.

For example, Chuy’s current nutrition information lists approximately:

Hatch Green Chile Sauce: 25 calories

Tangy Tomatillo: 60 calories

Roasted Ranchero: 60 calories

Creamy Green Chile: 170 calories

Boom-Boom Sauce: 200 calories

Queso: 230 calories

If your burrito, enchiladas, or flautas come with a specific sauce, make sure that preparation is reflected in your calculation.

Enchilada Calories Depend on the Filling and Sauce

Chuy’s enchiladas come in several different styles.

Cheese, seasoned ground beef, roasted chicken, veggie, and creamy green chile chicken enchiladas all contain different amounts of protein, carbohydrates, fat, and sodium.

The sauce matters too.

For example, current official nutrition information lists:

Veggie Enchiladas: about 340 calories

Deluxe Chicken Enchiladas: about 470 calories

Chicka-Chicka Boom-Boom Enchiladas: about 540 calories

Tex-Mex Enchiladas: about 630 calories

Those entrée values should still be checked carefully because Chuy’s notes that certain sides or sauces may not be included in the nutrition analysis for some menu entries.

Fajitas Are More Than the Protein

A Chuy’s fajita order includes several components.

The grilled chicken or beef is only the beginning. Tortillas, rice, beans, cheese, guacamole, pico de gallo, and other accompaniments can all contribute to the final total.

For a useful estimate, think of the meal as:

Protein + tortillas + rice + beans + toppings

If you eat only part of the tortillas or skip some of the sides, your actual intake may differ from the full restaurant serving.

Burritos Can Change With the Sauce on Top

Chuy’s burritos are another category where the finished preparation matters.

A Steak Burrito topped with Hatch Green Chile Sauce is nutritionally different from a burrito filled with another protein or covered with a richer sauce.

The current menu also includes a Tex-Mex Burrito Bowl built with rice, cheese, peppers and onions, corn salsa, pico de gallo, guacamole, lettuce, and fajita chicken or beef.

A bowl and a burrito should therefore not be treated as interchangeable simply because they use similar fillings.

Queso, Chips, and Creamy Jalapeño Belong in the Total

Chuy’s serves complimentary chips and salsa, and its FAQ also highlights Creamy Jalapeño as a popular addition.

These extras can be easy to forget because they may arrive before the main meal.

Queso and Creamy Jalapeño are especially important to count separately because they can add substantial calories, fat, and sodium.

The current NutritionSolver data, for example, lists a 4 oz Creamy Jalapeño serving at roughly 410 calories .

If you share chips, queso, or another appetizer, estimate the portion you actually eat rather than assigning the entire table serving to one person.

Combination Plates Need Extra Attention

Chuy’s serves several combination-style meals, including items such as Comida Deluxe, Chicken Flautas, Chuychanga, Taco & Enchilada combinations, and other house specialties.

These meals can combine tortillas, fried items, cheese, sauces, guacamole, rice, beans, and multiple proteins on one plate.

The current official nutrition chart lists Comida Deluxe at about 1,190 calories before certain side considerations , showing why a combo meal should be treated differently from a single taco or enchilada.

Use the exact entrée whenever possible rather than trying to estimate a combination from one component.

Sodium Can Be High Even When Calories Look Moderate

Calories are not the only useful comparison at Chuy’s.

Cheese, tortillas, seasoned meats, sauces, queso, beans, and combination dishes can contribute significant sodium.

For example, two entrées may have similar calories but substantially different sodium totals depending on the sauce and ingredients.

If sodium matters to you, compare it after accounting for the main dish, sauce, sides, and dips .
