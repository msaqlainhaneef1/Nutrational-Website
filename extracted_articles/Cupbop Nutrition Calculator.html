Cupbop Nutrition Calculator

Cupbop combines Korean BBQ-style proteins with rice, noodles, vegetables, and its signature sauces, so the nutrition of a bowl depends on more than the protein you choose.

Use the Cupbop Nutrition Calculator above to compare Bops, sauces, sides, and snacks, or combine several items to see the calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, and sodium in your complete order.

What Makes Up a Cupbop Bowl?

Cupbop bowls bring several components together in one cup.

Rather than thinking of the meal as chicken, beef, or pork alone, it is more useful to look at the complete combination of:

Rice and noodles

Vegetables

Protein

Sauce

Those components explain why Cupbop bowls can contain a significant amount of carbohydrates even when the main topping is meat.

For example, current NutritionSolver data lists the Kko Kko Bop at about 593 calories with 26.3g protein and 93.9g carbohydrates , while the Piggy Bop contains about 693 calories with 28g protein and 102.2g carbohydrates .

The full bowl matters, not just the meat on top.

Sauce Level Can Change More Than the Heat

Cupbop uses numbered sauces, and the sauce should be counted as part of the meal rather than treated as a calorie-free seasoning.

NutritionSolver currently contains separate nutrition entries for sauces from #1 through #10 , plus additional sauce options.

Those entries vary considerably in calories, carbohydrates, sugar, fat, and sodium.

For example, the current database shows some lower-numbered sauces contributing more than 200 calories per listed serving, while the #10 Sauce is much lower in calories but still contributes carbohydrates and sodium.

So when you choose your preferred sauce level, make sure the nutrition entry matches the sauce used on your bowl.

Protein Is Only One Part of a High-Protein Bowl

Several Cupbop bowls provide more than 20g of protein, but a higher-protein bowl can still contain substantial calories, carbohydrates, fat, or sodium.

The Krunch Bop , for example, currently provides about 31.3g of protein for 557 calories , while the Rock Bop provides a similar amount of protein at a much higher overall calorie total.

That makes protein-to-calorie comparison useful when deciding between bowls.

Sodium Can Add Up Quickly

Cupbop is also a menu where sodium deserves attention separately from calories.

Current NutritionSolver values include approximately:

Kko Kko Bop: 1,288mg sodium

Piggy Bop: 1,260mg

Mando Bop: 1,328mg

Snow Bop: 1,400mg

Sauces can add more sodium on top of the base meal.

Kimchi also contains relatively little energy but can contribute a meaningful amount of sodium. The current database lists the serving at about 30 calories and 690mg sodium .

This is a good example of why the lowest-calorie item is not automatically the lowest-sodium item.

K-Dawgs and Mandoo Are Separate From the Bowl

Cupbop also offers Korean-style sides and snacks.

Current NutritionSolver entries include Mandoo and several K-Dawg preparations.

A 3-piece Mandoo serving is currently listed at about 99 calories , while K-Dawg varieties are around 650 calories each in the existing database.

If you order one alongside a Bop, add it separately.

For example:

Kko Kko Bop + Mandoo

and

Kko Kko Bop + K-Dawg

have very different totals even though the main bowl is identical.
