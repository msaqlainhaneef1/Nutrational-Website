Subway Nutrition Calculator

A Subway sandwich does not have one universal nutrition total.

The final result depends on the sandwich recipe, 6-inch or Footlong size, bread, protein amount, cheese, vegetables, sauces, and extras you choose.

That makes Subway different from restaurants where the standard entrée rarely changes.

Use the Subway Nutrition Calculator above to build the exact sandwich or meal you order rather than relying on one number for every version of the same sub.

Choose 6-Inch or Footlong First

Size is one of the most important Subway nutrition choices.

Current Subway ordering pages separately calculate the:

6-inch

and:

Footlong

versions of the same sandwich.

For example, a current U.S. Oven-Roasted Turkey configuration at participating restaurants is shown at approximately:

6-inch: 470 calories

Footlong: 930 calories .

The Footlong is therefore not always best calculated by simply multiplying the 6-inch nutrition by two.

Use Subway's actual published size-specific result whenever it is available.

Bread Is Part of the Calculation

After choosing sandwich size, select the actual bread.

Current U.S. Subway ordering menus include bread options such as:

Artisan Italian

Flatbread

Hearty Multigrain

Italian Herbs & Cheese

and other location-dependent breads.

For one current Footlong ordering configuration, the bread components alone are listed at approximately:

Artisan Italian: 413 calories

Hearty Multigrain: 402 calories

Flatbread: 448 calories

Italian Herbs & Cheese: 497 calories .

Those values describe the bread component before considering the complete sandwich recipe.

The calculator should therefore connect bread choice to the selected sandwich rather than treating every sandwich as though it always uses the same bread.

Bread Values Also Change With Sandwich Size

The current 6-inch versions of those same bread choices are approximately:

Artisan Italian: 207 calories

Hearty Multigrain: 201 calories

Flatbread: 224 calories

Italian Herbs & Cheese: 249 calories .

That makes bread size another reason every Subway record needs an explicit serving size.

A card labeled only:

Italian Herbs & Cheese Bread

without saying 6-inch or Footlong is incomplete.

Start With the Standard Recipe Before Customizing

Subway currently sells both established sandwiches and more recipe-driven sandwiches.

A standard published sandwich can include:

protein

cheese

vegetables

sauce

and:

bread

before the customer makes any changes.

For example, a current Oven-Roasted Turkey configuration uses turkey, provolone, lettuce, tomato, onion, mayonnaise, and Artisan Italian bread.

If you use the complete published nutrition for that standard sandwich, do not add those standard components a second time.

Only adjust the total when the customer changes the recipe.

Extra Protein Needs Its Own Control

The current Subway ordering interface supports protein adjustments such as:

Regular

50% More Protein

and:

Double Protein

for compatible sandwiches.

This should be a real calculator option.

A customer ordering double turkey should not receive the same protein, calories, sodium, and other nutrition as the standard sandwich.

The best flow is:

Sandwich → Size → Protein Amount

before moving to cheese and toppings.

Subway Series Needs Recipe-Level Nutrition

Subway Series sandwiches are designed as named recipes rather than simply a meat selection.

Examples in recent U.S. nutrition/menu data include products such as:

The Beast

The Boss

The Monster

The Philly

and other specialty sandwiches.

A useful calculator should begin with the official recipe, then allow supported modifications.

It should not force the user to reconstruct a Subway Series sandwich from scratch.

Cheese Needs to Update the Sandwich

Different cheeses contribute different calories, fat, protein, and sodium.

If the standard recipe contains provolone and the customer removes it, the total should decrease.

If the customer changes to another cheese or adds extra cheese, the calculator should apply that specific portion.

Use:

standard cheese → remove / replace / extra

rather than simply:

cheese yes/no .

Vegetables Should Be Added in the Portions Subway Uses

Subway lets customers customize vegetables extensively.

Common U.S. choices include ingredients such as:

lettuce

tomatoes

red onions

green peppers

cucumbers

pickles

jalapeños

spinach

and additional location-dependent ingredients.

Although many vegetable portions contribute relatively few calories, they can still affect carbohydrates, fiber, and sodium.

The calculator should use Subway's actual serving portions rather than generic one-cup vegetable data.

Sauces Can Change the Sandwich Quickly

Subway sauces include calorie-containing choices such as mayonnaise, ranch-style sauces, aioli, honey mustard, sweet sauces, and other current dressings.

Several sauces can add a meaningful amount to a sandwich even though they are physically a small part of the order.

Choose the exact sauce and serving amount.

Sauce Should Not Be Added Twice

If a named Subway Series sandwich already includes a sauce in the official published recipe, that sauce is already represented in the standard nutrition.

Only add another portion if the customer orders:

extra sauce

or:

an additional second sauce .

This is especially important when a sandwich contains mayonnaise, ranch, aioli, or another energy-dense sauce.

Toasting Alone Should Not Create a New Nutrition Record

Toasting the same sandwich generally changes temperature and texture rather than adding a new ingredient.

A toasted and non-toasted version should therefore normally share the same nutrition unless an additional preparation ingredient is used.

Store:

Toasted: Yes/No

as an ordering preference rather than generating duplicate nutrition records unnecessarily.

Wraps Need Their Own Base

A Subway wrap is not simply a breadless 6-inch sandwich.

The tortilla itself contributes nutrition and can use a different amount of filling depending on the current recipe.

Choose:

Sub / Wrap / Salad / Bowl

where those formats are available.

Then calculate the appropriate base and filling portions for that format.

Salads and Bowls Should Not Automatically Use Sandwich Nutrition

Removing bread does not necessarily mean:

sandwich calories minus bread calories .

The protein and vegetable quantities used in salads or bowls can differ from sandwich portions.

Where Subway publishes a finished salad or bowl configuration, use that specific serving rather than subtracting bread mathematically.

A Footlong Is Not Always Exactly Double the 6-Inch

This deserves special attention.

The live Subway ordering system currently shows examples where the relationship is not a perfect 2× multiplication.

One current Oven-Roasted Turkey configuration is:

6-inch: 470 calories

Footlong: 930 calories .

Two times 470 would be 940.

That small difference demonstrates why published Footlong values are preferable to simply doubling every 6-inch record.

The same principle applies to protein, sauces, and other recipe components.

Subway Sides Are Changing Too

Subway's current U.S. side menu now includes Pretzel Twists .

Current live menu examples include approximately:

Original Pretzel Twist: 480 calories

Salted Pretzel Twist: 480 calories

Cinnamon Sugar Pretzel Twist: 510 calories .

The Original Pretzel Twist can also be paired with sauces such as Cheddar Cheese or Honey Mustard, which add their own nutrition.

This should be calculated as:

Pretzel Twist + selected dip

rather than one universal side total.

Pretzel Dip Can Add a Meaningful Amount

One current ordering page shows approximately:

Cheddar Cheese dip: 67 calories

and:

Honey Mustard: 177 calories

as optional additions to the Pretzel Twist.

If the customer uses the dip, add it.

If they eat the pretzel without sauce, do not include it automatically.

Footlong Cookie Needs Its Own Serving Context

Subway's Footlong Cookie is a large shareable-style dessert, not a normal single cookie.

The January 2026 U.S. nutrition table lists the Footlong Chocolate Chip Cookie at approximately:

Sidekick Availability Changes

Subway originally introduced Footlong Cookie, Footlong Churro, and Footlong Pretzel as Sidekicks.

Current U.S. store menus I reviewed now prominently show newer Pretzel Twist products instead.

That means old Sidekick products should have menu-status metadata rather than automatically remaining in the current national menu forever.

Use:

Current

Select Locations

Historical / Previous Sidekick

or:

Availability Unconfirmed .

Meal Nutrition Should Add the Side and Drink

A sandwich alone is not the same as a Subway meal.

Current ordering pages allow customers to add a side and drink.

The correct total is:

sandwich + selected side + exact drink .

If someone orders water instead of regular soda, the finished meal can differ substantially without changing the sub itself.

Build Subway in the Same Order You Order It

For sandwiches:

Choose Sandwich → 6-inch or Footlong → Bread → Protein Amount → Add-Ons → Cheese → Vegetables → Sauces → Seasonings

For another format:

Choose Wrap, Salad, or Bowl → Recipe → Protein → Cheese → Vegetables → Sauce

Then add:

Side → Dip → Cookie → Drink

For group-sized or Footlong desserts, choose the amount personally eaten rather than automatically treating the entire item as one person's serving.

Subway already provides unusually detailed recipe and customization information. The strongest calculator is therefore not a short table of sandwich names. It is a builder that preserves the exact serving size and updates the nutrition as the customer changes the order.
