Willy’s Mexicana Grill Nutrition Calculator

At Willy’s Mexicana Grill, the nutrition of your meal is decided one scoop at a time.

You choose the tortilla or bowl, then the protein, rice, beans, vegetables, cheese, guacamole, sour cream, salsa, and other toppings. You may also get chips and salsa with your entrée, add cheese dip, smother a burrito, or turn a basic quesadilla into a Grande.

That flexibility is part of what makes Willy’s appealing, but it also means there is no single nutrition number that represents every burrito, bowl, taco, or salad.

The Willy’s Mexicana Grill Nutrition Calculator helps you build the meal you actually plan to eat and estimate its calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, sodium, fiber, sugar, saturated fat, and other available nutrition values.

Think of Your Willy’s Order as Five Nutrition Layers

Instead of trying to estimate the entire meal at once, I find it easier to divide a Willy’s order into five parts:

  1. The base , such as a tortilla, bowl, greens, chips, or taco shells
  2. The protein , such as Sinaloa Chicken, Adobe Chicken, Ancho Steak, ground beef, or tofu
  3. The filling , such as rice and beans
  4. The toppings , such as cheese, guacamole, sour cream, pico, corn salsa, vegetables, or dressing
  5. The extras , such as chips, cheese dip, a drink, or dessert

Willy’s current menu is specifically designed around Build Your Own Burritos, Bowls, Salads, Quesadillas, Tacos, and Nachos, so this ingredient-by-ingredient approach closely matches how the food is actually ordered.

Choose Your Meal Format Before the Fillings

A burrito, bowl, salad, quesadilla, tacos, and nachos can contain many of the same ingredients, but they do not start with the same base.

That matters.

A burrito includes a large flour tortilla.

A bowl removes the tortilla and lets the rice, beans, protein, and toppings become the meal.

A salad starts with greens.

Tacos use individual flour, corn, or crispy shells.

A quesadilla combines a flour tortilla with protein and other fillings.

Nachos start with tortilla chips.

Before comparing chicken with steak or black beans with pinto beans, select the correct meal format.

Burrito vs Bowl

One of the simplest ways to change a Willy’s meal is choosing a bowl instead of a burrito.

A bowl can contain the same rice, beans, protein, vegetables, salsa, cheese, and guacamole without wrapping everything in the large tortilla. Willy’s officially describes its Build Your Own Bowl as the burrito ingredients without the tortilla, or with it served on the side.

Removing the tortilla can reduce calories and carbohydrates, but that does not automatically make every bowl low in calories.

A bowl with a large amount of rice, beans, cheese dip, sour cream, cheese, and guacamole can still become a substantial meal.

Calculate the complete combination.

Start Protein Comparisons With Sinaloa Chicken

Sinaloa Chicken is one of Willy’s signature proteins.

It uses a citrus-based marinade with orange juice, garlic, and spices. Willy’s currently lists a calorie range of roughly 118 to 150 calories depending on the serving context.

It is useful as a baseline when comparing the other proteins because you can then see what changes when you move to spicy chicken, steak, beef, or tofu.

If protein is important to you, compare grams of protein together with total calories rather than focusing only on which meat sounds leanest.

Adobe Chicken Gives You a Spicier Chicken Choice

Adobe Chicken uses roasted ancho chilies and jalapeños and currently falls into a slightly different calorie range from Sinaloa Chicken.

This is a good example of why similar ingredients should still have separate calculator entries.

Both are chicken.

Both can go inside the same burrito.

But seasoning, preparation, portioning, and recipe differences mean you should select the exact protein you order.

Ancho Steak Changes the Meal Again

Willy’s Ancho Steak uses Certified Angus Beef seasoned with an ancho chile dry rub. The restaurant currently lists approximately 141 to 180 calories for its stated serving range.

Steak gives you another protein option without requiring you to change the rest of the meal.

Try building the same bowl twice in the calculator:

Version 1: Sinaloa Chicken

Version 2: Ancho Steak

Keep the rice, beans, cheese, salsa, and vegetables the same.

That gives you a much cleaner comparison than looking at two completely different entrées.

Do Not Overlook Sinaloa Tofu

Willy’s also offers marinated tofu.

The tofu is prepared with orange juice, garlic, and spices and provides a meat-free protein option. Willy’s official nutrition information currently shows a higher calorie range for the tofu serving than several of its meat proteins, which is a useful reminder that vegetarian does not automatically mean lower calorie.

Choose tofu because it fits your preferences, then calculate it like any other protein.

Ground Beef Has Its Own Recipe

Willy’s ground beef is not simply plain browned beef.

It is slow-cooked with ingredients including fire-roasted tomatoes, onion, garlic, cumin, and cayenne.

That recipe gives it a different nutrition profile from steak or chicken.

If you switch proteins, update the calculator even if everything else stays the same.

Rice Can Be One of the Bigger Carbohydrate Contributors

Willy’s offers vegetarian rice as part of many burritos and bowls, and its official ingredient information lists rice at roughly 100 to 133 calories depending on portion context.

Rice also contributes a significant share of the carbohydrates in many orders.

If you want to meaningfully change the carbohydrate content of a bowl, modifying the rice portion can have a larger effect than removing lettuce, pico, cucumber, or serranos.

Black Beans vs Pinto Beans

Willy’s prepares both black and pinto beans without meat products, making them vegetarian options.

Beans contribute more than calories.

They can also provide:

  • Carbohydrates
  • Fiber
  • Plant protein
  • Minerals

If you are comparing black and pinto beans, consider the complete nutrition profile rather than asking only which has fewer calories.

Rice and Beans Together Make a Difference

A burrito with rice and beans contains carbohydrates from several sources before the tortilla is even considered.

That does not mean rice and beans should be avoided.

It means they need to be included in the total.

If you are tracking carbohydrates closely, calculate:

Tortilla + rice + beans

before assuming the protein and toppings are the largest contributors.

Cheese Dip Is One of the Most Important Additions to Count

Willy’s cheese dip is currently listed at around 150 calories for its stated serving.

That makes it very different from a few tablespoons of pico de gallo.

Cheese dip can appear:

  • Inside an entrée
  • On top of a smothered burrito
  • With chips
  • As part of specialty recipes
  • As a separate side

If you have it in more than one place, make sure your estimate reflects the amount you actually eat.

Smothering a Burrito Changes the Calculation

Willy’s currently promotes smothered burritos with cheese dip and enchilada sauce.

That turns a regular handheld burrito into a different prepared meal.

Do not calculate the base burrito and ignore what goes on top.

The cheese dip and sauce become part of the entrée.

This matters especially with specialty burritos such as the Baja Burrito, which is grilled and smothered with enchilada sauce and cheese dip.

The Baja Burrito Needs Its Own Nutrition Estimate

The Baja Burrito combines chicken or steak with Monterey Jack cheese and sautéed peppers and onions before being grilled and smothered.

That makes it structurally different from a basic Build Your Own Burrito.

The protein choice still matters, but so do:

  • Cheese
  • Peppers and onions
  • Enchilada sauce
  • Cheese dip
  • Sides served with the meal

Select the specialty item when available rather than using a generic chicken burrito estimate.

Willy Philly Burrito Is Another Distinct Build

The current Willy Philly Burrito combines steak, rice, cheese dip, cheddar cheese, peppers and onions, and serrano crema.

Notice how many of those ingredients are calorie-dense compared with simple vegetable toppings.

This is why specialty burritos deserve their own calculation.

A burrito that contains cheese dip and crema can differ substantially from one built with steak, beans, lettuce, and pico.

Frito Burrito Adds Crunch and Calories Differently

The current Willy’s Frito Burrito includes Adobe Chicken, Frito chips, rice, pinto beans, sour cream, chipotle puree, and cheese dip.

The chips inside the burrito create another calorie and carbohydrate source.

It is a useful reminder that crunchy toppings still count even when they are inside the entrée rather than served on the side.

Goal Bowl Takes a Simpler Approach

Willy’s Goal Bowl currently combines lettuce, Adobe Chicken, pico de gallo, guacamole, and cucumbers.

Compared with many fully loaded burritos, it uses a much simpler set of ingredients.

That makes it useful for someone who wants to see more clearly what each major component contributes.

It also shows that you do not necessarily need to remove every higher-calorie ingredient. Guacamole can remain part of a relatively simple bowl.

Guacamole Is Not Just a Calorie Addition

Willy’s makes its guacamole with avocado, jalapeños, lime juice, and cilantro, and currently lists approximately 40 calories for its stated portion.

Avocado contributes fat and calories, but it can also contribute fiber and unsaturated fat.

If guacamole is one of your favorite ingredients, there may be little reason to remove it while keeping other toppings you care about less.

Use the calculator to decide which combinations work for you.

Sour Cream Has a Larger Impact Than Many Fresh Toppings

Willy’s currently lists sour cream at approximately 110 calories for its stated portion.

Compare that with:

  • Pico de gallo at roughly 15 calories
  • Cucumbers at roughly 5 calories
  • Lettuce starting at only a few calories
  • Serranos at essentially zero in the listed serving
  • Pickled jalapeños at essentially zero in the listed serving

The lesson is simple.

If you want to meaningfully change calories, start with the ingredients that contribute the most.

Pico de Gallo Adds Flavor With Relatively Few Calories

Willy’s pico combines tomatoes, cilantro, jalapeños, onions, lime juice, and salt and is currently listed at around 15 calories per stated portion.

This makes pico very different nutritionally from cheese dip or sour cream.

It is a good example of a topping that can contribute flavor and freshness without substantially changing the calorie total.

Corn Salsa Is Still Worth Counting

Corn salsa is more substantial than pico because corn contributes carbohydrates.

Willy’s currently lists it at approximately 35 calories for the stated serving.

That is still modest compared with many creamy toppings, but if you are tracking carbohydrates closely, it belongs in the calculation.

Cheese Portions Can Vary a Lot

Willy’s ingredient information shows a broad calorie range for cheese depending on the serving and menu application.

That makes portion context especially important.

A small amount of Monterey Jack used as one burrito topping is not nutritionally equivalent to a cheese-heavy quesadilla.

Do not use a single cheese value for every menu format.

Quesadillas Are Naturally Cheese-Centered

A Build Your Own Quesadilla starts with a flour tortilla and can include protein before being grilled. Willy’s currently serves it with pico de gallo and sour cream, while the Grande version uses a larger tortilla, more ingredients, and includes guacamole.

That creates several nutrition differences at once:

  • More tortilla
  • More filling
  • More cheese
  • Added guacamole
  • Potentially more protein

Make sure you choose the standard or Grande version that matches your actual order.

Tacos Make Portion Counting Easier

Willy’s Build Your Own Tacos currently come as two tacos with a choice of soft flour, soft corn, or crispy corn tortillas.

That gives you two useful decisions:

How many tacos am I eating?

and

Which shell am I choosing?

A flour tortilla, soft corn tortilla, and fried crispy shell do not necessarily have the same nutrition.

Calculate the version you actually order.

Nachos Begin With Chips, Not a Tortilla

Willy’s nachos use homemade tortilla chips as the base and can be topped with beans, salsa, sour cream, guacamole, and your chosen additions.

This means nachos should not be treated as a burrito without the tortilla.

The chips contribute their own calories, carbohydrates, fat, and sodium.

Then every topping is added on top of that base.

Free Chips and Salsa Still Count

This is one of the most Willy’s-specific parts of the meal.

Willy’s currently includes chips and salsa with entrée purchases.

Because the chips arrive automatically, it is easy to think of them as separate from the meal.

Nutritionally, they are still food you may consume.

If you eat the chips, include them.

If you eat only part of the portion, estimate the amount you personally consumed.

Chips Are Easy to Eat Without Tracking the Portion

A few chips can turn into half a basket surprisingly quickly.

If you share them, estimating your personal portion will never be perfectly precise.

That is okay.

You can use a reasonable fraction.

If two people split the chips roughly evenly, estimate half.

If four people share them evenly, estimate roughly one quarter.

A sensible estimate is more useful than pretending the chips were calorie-free.

Salsa Choices Change Flavor More Than Calories

Salsa Can Matter for Allergens

Calories are not the only reason to know which salsa you choose.

Willy’s currently states that sesame seeds are present in Chili D’Arbol, Chipotle, and Habanero salsas as well as cheese dip.

For someone with a sesame allergy, that information can be much more important than the calorie number.

Always treat allergen information separately from general nutrition tracking.

Taco Salad Is Not Simply Lettuce and Meat

That is a full entrée.

The fried shell, cheese dip, sour cream, beef, beans, and guacamole all contribute to the nutrition.

Southwest Chicken Salad Has a Different Structure

Dressing Can Change a Build Your Own Salad

Drinks Can Quietly Increase the Meal Total

Willy’s current beverage menu includes fountain soda, iced tea, homemade lemonade, bottled soft drinks, Snapple, Mexican Coke and Fanta, Topo Chico, and water.

Homemade Lemonade Should Not Be Treated Like Water

Fresh lemonade may sound lighter than soda, but it can still contain added sugar.

Vegetarian at Willy’s Is Relatively Easy to Build

Willy’s states that its rice and beans are vegetarian and that it does not use lard in its recipes.

That gives you several ways to create meat-free burritos, bowls, salads, tacos, and other meals.

Tofu provides another meat-free protein option.

Remember that vegetarian does not automatically mean low calorie.

Cheese, cheese dip, sour cream, guacamole, tortillas, chips, and dressings still contribute to the final nutrition.

Vegan Orders Require More Attention to Toppings

Willy’s states that vegan eating is possible when meat and items containing dairy and eggs are avoided.

Dairy is currently identified in items including cheese dip, sour cream, Monterey Jack cheese, and certain dressings. Eggs are present in cookies and certain ranch dressings.

A vegan bowl might therefore focus on ingredients such as:

  • Rice
  • Beans
  • Tofu
  • Lettuce
  • Pico
  • Corn salsa
  • Guacamole
  • Vegetables
  • Appropriate salsa

Gluten-Friendly Does Not Mean Zero Cross-Contact

Willy’s offers gluten-friendly options but explicitly notes that food may not be entirely gluten-free because it is prepared in the same kitchen as gluten-containing foods.

That distinction matters.

Someone choosing gluten-friendly food as a preference has a different level of concern from someone with celiac disease or a severe wheat allergy.

For medical gluten avoidance, confirm preparation practices directly with the restaurant.

Nutrition Information Disclaimer

Willy’s Mexicana Grill nutrition values are estimates based on published restaurant information, standard serving sizes, and recipes.

Because Willy’s meals are highly customizable, actual nutrition can change with ingredient quantities, protein portions, rice and bean servings, tortillas, cheese, cheese dip, guacamole, sour cream, dressings, sauces, chips, and other modifications.

Restaurant preparation, recipe updates, regional availability, limited-time products, and portion variation can also affect the meal you receive.

Allergen labels should not be treated as guarantees against cross-contact. Willy’s states that gluten-friendly food is prepared in shared kitchens, and the restaurant publishes specific allergen information for dairy, eggs, nuts, soy, sesame, and other concerns.

Use the calculator as a practical tool for comparing choices and estimating your meal rather than as an exact laboratory measurement. Anyone with a severe food allergy, celiac disease, or a medical condition requiring specific nutrition management should confirm current information with the restaurant and seek individualized professional guidance where appropriate.
