Moe’s Southwest Grill Nutrition Calculator

Moe’s nutrition is easiest to understand when you build the meal the same way you order it.

Instead of starting with one fixed burrito number, think about the tortilla or bowl, rice, beans, protein, cheese, queso, guacamole, vegetables, salsa, sauces, and extras you actually choose.

Then there is one important Moe’s detail that is easy to forget: chips and salsa come with meals at participating locations . If you eat them, they belong in your meal calculation too.

Use the Moe’s Nutrition Calculator above to assemble the order you actually eat rather than relying on one generic calorie estimate.

Rice Can Become a Significant Part of the Bowl

Moe’s bowls can contain a larger rice portion than a standard burrito.

That means removing the tortilla does not automatically tell you how many calories you saved.

If the bowl contains more rice or additional toppings, the difference may be smaller than expected.

For the best estimate:

Choose entrée format → choose exact rice portion → add the remaining ingredients.

Black Beans and Pinto Beans Are Separate Choices

Moe’s offers black and pinto beans as separate ingredients.

They can be similar in calories, but their sodium and other nutrition values may differ.

If you choose both beans, count both portions.

If you skip beans, do not leave a default bean serving in the calculation.

Homewrecker Needs Customization Context

The Homewrecker remains one of Moe’s recognizable fan favorites. Current official location pages continue to feature it prominently.

But the finished nutrition still depends on choices such as protein and other customizations.

For that reason, a useful Homewrecker calculator should work as:

Homewrecker base → protein → rice and beans → optional extras

rather than showing one universal calorie number as though every Homewrecker is identical.

The Stack Is Its Own Entrée

The Stack should not be calculated as a burrito.

Its structure combines tortillas and a crunchy component with fillings and queso, creating a different base from either a burrito or a taco.

The current official brand continues to feature the Stack as a fan-favorite menu choice.

If you customize the protein or toppings, apply those changes to the Stack recipe rather than starting from burrito nutrition.

Nachos Begin With a Large Chip Base

Nachos deserve special attention because tortilla chips form the base of the entrée.

Then toppings such as meat, beans, cheese, queso, sour cream, guac, salsa, and vegetables are added.

This means a nacho calculation should start:

Nacho chip portion

then add:

protein + beans + queso/cheese + toppings

Do not start with a burrito and simply remove the tortilla.

Small add-on

Entrée ingredient

Side serving

Larger shareable serving

Those are not interchangeable.

NutritionSolver currently contains multiple distinct queso portion records, which is the correct concept. The user should see the portion name clearly before adding it to a meal.

If queso is already included in an entrée recipe, only add another serving when you actually order extra queso.

Guacamole Uses the Same Portion Logic

Guacamole can also be used as an entrée add-on or ordered as a separate side.

A small amount inside a burrito should not receive the nutrition for an entire side cup.

Use the exact portion that corresponds to the order.

This matters particularly for fat and total calories because avocado-based guacamole can contribute meaningful energy even when the serving looks relatively small.

Free Chips Are Not Nutritionally Free

This is one of the biggest differences between a Moe’s order and many other fast-casual meals.

Moe’s advertises free tortilla chips and salsa with meals at participating restaurants.

They may be free in price, but they still have calories.

If you eat chips while waiting for your burrito, dip them in queso, and then eat the entrée, all of those foods belong in the same meal total.

For a realistic estimate, select the amount of chips you personally eat rather than automatically counting an entire shared basket.

Chips Plus Queso Can Become a Separate Mini-Meal

The combination of chips and queso can add considerably more than salsa alone.

That makes these three orders very different:

Burrito only

Burrito + chips and salsa

Burrito + chips and queso

The main entrée has not changed, but the complete meal has.

This is why the meal builder should keep sides and dips separate from the burrito itself.

Salsa Should Be Chosen by Type

Moe’s is known for salsa options and seasonal salsas.

Different salsa recipes can contain different amounts of tomato, corn, vegetables, seasonings, or other ingredients.

Although salsa generally contributes less than queso or guacamole, it should still be represented by the actual salsa when verified data is available.

For multiple trips to the salsa bar, count the approximate amount you actually consume.

Moe’s currently offers a Moe Value Meal consisting of a Junior Burrito, a 2 oz side of queso, and a regular fountain drink, with free chips, salsa, and dipping sauces also associated with the offer.

That is a good example of why entrée calories alone are not meal calories.

A complete calculation needs:

Junior Burritos Need Junior Ingredient Portions

A Junior Burrito is not simply a regular burrito with the final calorie number cut in half.

Moe’s nutrition data uses smaller ingredient portions specifically for Junior Burritos.

For example, NutritionSolver already stores separate Junior portions for rice and beans.

That is the correct architecture.

Use the smaller recipe portions directly rather than mathematically dividing a full burrito.

Kids Meals Should Remain Separate

Moe’s also offers kids menu formats such as burritos, tacos, and quesadillas.

These use different serving sizes from adult entrées.

A Kids Burrito should therefore keep its own portion logic for tortilla, meat, rice, beans, cheese, and other included ingredients.

Do not calculate it from the standard adult burrito.

One Extra Ingredient Can Have a Very Different Impact From Another

Customizing with more lettuce, cilantro, cucumbers, or jalapeños is not nutritionally equivalent to adding more queso, cheese, guacamole, meat, or sour cream.

This makes ingredient-level data useful.

Instead of asking only:

How many toppings did I add?

ask:

Which toppings did I add, and how much of each?
