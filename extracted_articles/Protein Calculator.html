How Much Protein Do You Need Per Day?

Protein needs are not the same for everyone. Your body weight matters, but so do your activity level and what you are trying to achieve.

The Protein Calculator above estimates your daily protein target in grams using your weight, activity level, and fitness goal. It also gives you a useful range rather than treating one number as a perfect target.

That makes the result easier to use in real life, where protein requirements naturally vary from person to person.

Protein Needs Are Usually Based on Body Weight

Daily protein recommendations are commonly expressed as grams of protein per kilogram of body weight .

For example, a person who weighs 75 kg and uses a target of 1.6 g/kg would calculate:

75 × 1.6 = 120 grams of protein per day

The appropriate amount can change depending on how active you are.

For generally healthy adults with low activity, the established Recommended Dietary Allowance is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day . People who exercise regularly often use higher protein targets because training increases the demand for muscle repair and adaptation.

The calculator does this math for you and adjusts the estimate according to the activity level and goal you select.

Why Your Activity Level Changes the Result

Someone who spends most of the day sitting does not necessarily need the same protein target as someone who regularly strength trains or performs demanding exercise.

Research on exercising adults commonly supports protein intakes above the basic adult allowance. For many people who train regularly, a range around 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg per day is commonly used as a practical reference.

That does not mean more protein is always better. Once your needs are being met, simply adding more protein does not automatically produce more muscle or better results.

Your training, overall diet, calorie intake, sleep, and recovery still matter.

Understanding Your Protein Target

If your result says 130 grams per day , you do not need to consume exactly 130 grams every single day for the calculator to be useful.

Treat the number as a practical daily target.

A simple way to make that target easier to reach is to divide it across the meals you already eat. Someone aiming for 120 grams, for example, might spread protein across three or four meals rather than trying to consume most of it at once.

You can then use nutrition labels or NutritionSolver's food data to see how much protein different foods contribute toward your daily total.

Protein for Muscle Gain and Training

Eating enough protein supports the repair and maintenance of muscle tissue, but protein by itself does not build muscle.

Resistance training provides the stimulus for muscle growth, while adequate protein and total energy help support the body's response to that training.

This distinction is important. A protein calculator can estimate an appropriate intake, but it cannot replace the training and recovery factors that influence muscle development.

Protein During a Calorie Deficit

Protein can also become more important when calorie intake is reduced, particularly for people who want to maintain lean mass while losing weight.

However, the right intake still depends on the individual. A larger number is not automatically a better number, and very high protein targets can make it harder to leave enough room in the diet for carbohydrates, fats, fiber, and micronutrient-rich foods.

The goal is to find a protein intake that fits your needs, not simply to maximize protein.

Your calculated protein target is an estimate designed to make daily protein planning easier.
