Chipotle Nutrition Calculator

At Chipotle, your ingredients are your nutrition facts.

A burrito with white rice, black beans, chicken, cheese, sour cream, and guacamole has a very different nutrition profile from a bowl built with greens, steak, fajita vegetables, and salsa.

Use the Chipotle Nutrition Calculator above to add the ingredients you actually order and see the combined calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, sodium, fiber, and other available nutrition values.

Burrito or Bowl? The Tortilla Makes a Real Difference

A burrito and burrito bowl can contain exactly the same fillings, but the burrito adds a large flour tortilla.

Chipotle's standard burrito tortilla contains about 320 calories , along with carbohydrates, fat, and sodium.

A bowl removes that tortilla from the starting point, but that does not automatically make every bowl low in calories. Rice, double protein, cheese, sour cream, queso, and guacamole can still create a substantial meal.

The best comparison is therefore not simply “burrito vs bowl.” Compare the ingredients inside each one.

Build the Calories Ingredient by Ingredient

Chipotle makes this especially easy because most of the menu is assembled from individual components.

A practical way to calculate your order is:

Entrée format → rice → beans → protein → salsa → toppings → extras

For example, begin with a burrito tortilla or bowl, then add your chosen rice and beans. After that, select your protein, salsa, cheese, sour cream, guacamole, queso, or vegetables.

Every selection contributes independently to the final total.

This makes ingredient-level calculation more useful than relying on an average “Chipotle bowl calories” number.

Rice and Beans Change Both Carbs and Fiber

Chipotle's Cilantro-Lime White Rice and Brown Rice are both about 210 calories per standard serving , but their carbohydrate and fiber profiles are not identical.

Black Beans and Pinto Beans add carbohydrates too, while also contributing protein and dietary fiber.

If you order both rice and beans, count both. If you ask for extra portions, your actual meal can contain more than the standard values displayed for one serving.

For a more accurate estimate, build the bowl as closely as possible to the portions you receive.

Protein Choice Changes More Than Protein

Chicken, steak, barbacoa, carnitas, Sofritas, and limited-time proteins each have their own calorie and macro profiles.

For example, standard Chipotle chicken provides more protein per serving than Sofritas, while carnitas naturally contain more fat than some of the leaner protein choices.

Chipotle also rotates limited-time proteins. In 2026, Chipotle Honey Chicken returned to the menu, while Chicken al Pastor also returned earlier in the year.

When a seasonal protein is available, use its own nutrition entry rather than substituting standard chicken.

Guacamole and Queso Need to Be Counted Separately

Guacamole and Queso Blanco can make a noticeable difference to the total meal.

A standard serving of guacamole is about 230 calories . Queso Blanco nutrition depends on whether you are looking at an entrée portion or a larger side portion.

This serving-size distinction matters.

If you add guacamole inside your bowl and also order chips and guacamole on the side, those are separate portions and should not be counted as one.

Salsa Is Not Just About Calories

Chipotle's salsas are relatively modest in calories, but their sodium values can differ considerably.

Fresh Tomato Salsa, Roasted Chili-Corn Salsa, Tomatillo-Green Chili Salsa, and Tomatillo-Red Chili Salsa each have a different nutrition profile.

If you are comparing a meal by sodium rather than calories alone, the specific salsa can therefore matter.

The same is especially true for the Chipotle-Honey Vinaigrette , which contributes its own calories, fat, carbohydrates, sugar, and sodium and should be included when you use it with a salad or bowl.

Double Protein Means Double the Protein Portion

If you order double chicken, double steak, or another double protein, do not calculate it using a single serving.

Add the protein portion twice.

This increases protein, but also increases the calories, fat, sodium, and other nutrients associated with that protein.

Chipotle has increasingly highlighted high-protein combinations and currently offers protein-focused menu options, but the complete nutrition still depends on everything else added to the entrée.

Chips Can Change the Order More Than Expected

A standard serving of Chipotle chips contains about 540 calories .

That means:

Bowl only

and

Bowl + chips + guacamole

are very different orders nutritionally, even though the bowl itself has not changed.

Chips with Queso Blanco or guacamole should therefore be calculated as a separate side rather than assumed to be part of the entrée nutrition.

Tacos Need the Correct Tortilla Count

Chipotle tacos can use crispy corn or soft flour tortillas.

The nutrition depends on both the tortilla type and the number of tacos you eat.

If you order three tacos, make sure the tortilla quantity and fillings reflect all three rather than calculating nutrition for only one taco.

The fillings matter too. Three tacos with chicken, cheese, and sour cream will not have the same total as three tacos filled with Sofritas, vegetables, and salsa.
