Brassica Nutrition Guide

Brassica is different from restaurant chains that publish fixed calorie and macro values for every menu item.

The restaurant uses locally sourced, seasonal, and artisan ingredients in made-from-scratch recipes. Because ingredients and recipes can change throughout the year, Brassica does not currently publish complete tested calorie and nutrition information for its menu .

That means an exact calorie number for a Brassica sandwich or salad should not be presented as verified unless it comes from current restaurant testing or another clearly identified reliable source.

How a Brassica Meal Is Built

Brassica's menu centers on customizable sandwiches and salads , allowing you to combine several ingredients into one meal.

A typical order can include:

Base: organic whole wheat pita or greens

Protein: options such as chicken, beef, or falafel

Spreads: hummus, baba, and similar components

Vegetables: cabbage, cucumbers, radishes, cauliflower, eggplant, beets, carrots, and other seasonal choices

Sauces: tahini, roasted red pepper sauce, z'hug, and other house sauces

Because each combination is different, two Brassica orders can have very different nutrition profiles even when they are both described simply as a salad or sandwich.

Why Brassica Calories Are Difficult to Calculate Exactly

Many restaurant nutrition calculators rely on standardized recipes that remain almost identical across every location.

Brassica operates differently.

The restaurant says its local and seasonal sourcing requires frequent changes to ingredients, recipes, and producers. According to Brassica, accurately publishing complete nutrition information would therefore require repeated nutritional testing whenever those recipes change.

For that reason, any third-party Brassica calorie calculator should clearly distinguish between verified nutrition data and estimates .

Sandwich vs Salad

Choosing a sandwich or salad changes the foundation of the meal.

A sandwich includes Brassica's organic whole wheat pita, while a salad uses greens as the base. From there, proteins, spreads, vegetables, sauces, and additional ingredients determine the rest of the meal.

The pita therefore should not be treated as nutritionally equivalent to a salad base.

Likewise, adding hummus, falafel, meat, fries, tahini, or another sauce changes the final meal beyond the base alone.

Do Sauces and Extras Matter?

Yes.

Ingredients such as hummus, tahini-based sauces, fries, falafel, and other additions contribute their own calories and nutrients.

Brassica also offers separate menu items such as Brassica Fries, Brassarita, and a Tahini Chocolate Chip Cookie , which should be treated separately from the sandwich or salad if nutrition information becomes available.
