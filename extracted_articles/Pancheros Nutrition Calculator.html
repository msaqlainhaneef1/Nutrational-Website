Pancheros Nutrition Calculator

Pancheros builds a burrito differently from most fast-casual Mexican chains.

The restaurant makes its flour tortillas from dough in-house, presses them fresh to order, fills them with the ingredients you choose, and then mixes those fillings before rolling the burrito. Pancheros calls this its “perfectly mixed” approach.

From a nutrition perspective, however, the calculation is still easiest when you separate that perfectly mixed burrito back into its individual parts.

Use the Pancheros Nutrition Calculator above to choose your entrée, tortilla or base, rice, beans, protein, cheese, queso, guacamole, vegetables, salsa, dressing, and sides.

The Fresh-Pressed Tortilla Is a Major Part of the Burrito

Pancheros makes its tortillas in-house rather than using a small prepackaged wrap.

The nutrition sheet currently linked from the official Pancheros menu lists approximately:

Large Tortilla: 350 calories

Small Tortilla: 80 calories.

That makes the large tortilla one of the biggest single calorie contributors in a standard burrito before rice, beans, meat, cheese, or queso are added.

This is why a Burrito and Burrito Bowl should not automatically share the same nutrition total.

The Bowl Removes the Tortilla, Not the Rest of the Burrito

That means a bowl is not automatically a low-calorie meal.

Rice Adds More Than a Small Garnish

The nutrition PDF currently linked by Pancheros lists one standard Cilantro Lime Rice portion at approximately:

210 calories

39g carbohydrates

4g protein

800mg sodium .

This makes rice a substantial part of the meal, especially when combined with the tortilla in a burrito.

If you skip rice, remove it from the build rather than leaving it in a standard burrito estimate.

Black and Pinto Beans Need Separate Entries

Black Beans

and

The linked nutrition sheet lists a serving of each at about 60 calories, but their carbohydrate, fiber, and protein values are not identical.

Select the bean you actually order.

Pancheros Has a Naming Problem Around Its Plant-Based Protein

This deserves special attention.

The current live menu calls the plant-based protein “Tofurizo.”

However, Pancheros' linked nutrition PDF and allergen information still use the older name Tofusada® .

NutritionSolver should not show these as two unrelated proteins unless Pancheros confirms that they are genuinely different recipes.

A cleaner current label would be:

Tofurizo, previously listed as Tofusada in Pancheros nutrition materials

until the company updates or clarifies the official documentation.

Queso Can Be One of the Largest Additions

Queso is much more than a small salsa topping.

Pancheros' currently linked nutrition sheet provides two serving contexts:

Queso, 1.9 oz: about 190 calories

Queso, 5 oz: about 390 calories.

The larger serving also contains approximately 31g fat and 1,480mg sodium .

This makes portion labeling essential.

A little queso added inside a burrito and a large side of queso should never receive the same nutrition value.

Guacamole Also Needs the Correct Portion

Pancheros publishes different guacamole portions as well.

Its linked nutrition sheet lists approximately:

Guacamole, 2.1 oz: 70 calories

Guacamole, 5.5 oz: 190 calories.

The smaller portion makes sense as an entrée addition, while the larger serving can represent a much more substantial side.

NutritionSolver should display the portion beside the ingredient name.

Shredded Cheese Can Add More Than Expected

The official nutrition sheet currently linked by Pancheros lists:

Shredded Cheese, 0.6 oz: about 70 calories

and a much larger:

2.3 oz serving: about 260 calories.

Once again, context matters.

A cheese serving used inside an entrée should not automatically be replaced by a large side or catering-style portion.

Salsa Heat Does Not Tell You the Calories

The current menu labels Mild Ancho, Salsa Verde, Hot Tomatillo, Roasted Corn, and Pico de Gallo with different heat levels.

Spicier does not automatically mean higher in calories.

For example, the linked nutrition data places many salsa servings quite low in calories, while a corn-based salsa can contribute more.

Choose salsa by its actual recipe and serving rather than its heat level.

Chile Lime Vinaigrette Makes the Salad a Different Meal

If you use the standard dressing, include it.

Tacos Use Two Fresh-Pressed Tortillas

Pancheros currently serves its taco entrée as two tacos on fresh-pressed soft flour tortillas with the customer's selected protein, lettuce, cheese, and salsa.

That serving structure should be visible in the calculator.

Use:

Tacos, 2 count

then add:

protein + cheese + salsa + toppings

rather than calculating a single taco and assuming it represents the entire menu order.

Quesadilla Is a Cheese-Forward Build

Pancheros describes its Quesadilla as a large fresh-grilled flour tortilla filled with cheese and the customer's selected protein, with salsa on the side.

That makes the quesadilla fundamentally different from a burrito.

It starts with:

Tortilla + cheese + protein

rather than tortilla, rice, beans, and multiple toppings by default.

Use its own build logic.

Queso, Guac, and Salsa Dips Should Stay Separate

The current menu specifically promotes chips with:

Queso

Homemade salsa

Fresh-made guacamole .

A chips-and-dip calculator should therefore work as:

Chips → choose dip → choose dip serving

rather than assigning every order the same total.

This is especially important because the published queso and guacamole servings vary widely in size.

“Perfectly Mixed” Does Not Change the Nutrition Total

Pancheros uses its proprietary mixing tool to distribute the ingredients through a burrito before rolling it.

Mixing changes how the ingredients are distributed across each bite.

It does not mean you should count the ingredients differently.

The nutrition total is still the sum of the tortilla, rice, beans, protein, cheese, salsa, and other selected components.
