Buffalo Wild Wings Nutrition Calculator

At Buffalo Wild Wings, the nutrition of your wings is not determined by the chicken alone. Wing type, quantity, sauce or dry rub, dipping sauce, and sides can all change the final calories and macros.

Use the Buffalo Wild Wings Nutrition Calculator above to build the order you actually plan to eat and see its combined calories, protein, carbs, fat, sodium, and other available nutrition values.

Build Your Wing Order in Three Steps

For the most useful estimate, think about your order in three parts:

1. Choose your wings
Select bone-in or boneless wings and the quantity that matches your order.

2. Add the flavor
Choose the sauce or dry seasoning applied to the wings.

3. Add dips and sides
Include ranch, bleu cheese, fries, potato wedges, vegetables, or any other items you plan to eat.

This gives you a much more realistic total than checking only the calories for the plain wings.

Bone-In and Boneless Wings Have Different Nutrition

Traditional bone-in wings and boneless wings are not interchangeable nutritionally.

Buffalo Wild Wings describes its bone-in wings as traditional Buffalo-style chicken wings, while its boneless wings use all-white chicken that is lightly breaded .

That breading means boneless wings generally contain more carbohydrates than traditional wings before a sauce is added.

For example, NutritionSolver currently lists a 6-count boneless wing serving at 360 calories, 29g protein, 20g carbs, and 19g fat before considering the selected flavor separately .

Always select the correct wing style instead of using one wing value for both.

Wing Count Matters

A six-wing order and a fifteen-wing order obviously do not contain the same nutrition, even if both use the same flavor.

When comparing Buffalo Wild Wings calories, make sure the serving in the calculator matches the number of wings you plan to eat.

If several people are sharing a larger order, calculate your own approximate portion rather than assigning the nutrition for the entire order to one person.

The Sauce Can Change More Than the Heat Level

Buffalo Wild Wings currently offers a wide range of sauces and dry seasonings, and their nutrition profiles vary considerably.

For example, current NutritionSolver data shows:

Asian Zing: 130 calories and 31g carbs
Honey BBQ: 100 calories and 25g carbs
Mango Habanero: 110 calories and 26g carbs
Original Buffalo: 170 calories and 18g fat
Lemon Pepper: 230 calories and 23g fat

These flavors clearly do more than change how spicy the wings taste.

Sweeter sauces can contribute more carbohydrates and sugar, while buttery or oil-based sauces can contribute considerably more fat.

Dry Rubs Are Worth Comparing Separately

Buffalo Wild Wings also offers dry seasonings such as Lemon Pepper, Desert Heat, Buffalo, Chipotle BBQ, and Salt & Vinegar.

A dry rub and a wet sauce can produce very different nutrition totals even when they are used on the same number of wings.

If the goal is to understand the full order, treat the flavor as a separate component rather than assuming every sauce or seasoning adds roughly the same amount.

Sodium Can Become a Major Part of a Wing Order

Buffalo Wild Wings is a good example of why calories should not be the only number you check.

Sauces can contribute substantial sodium on top of the sodium already present in the wings.

Current NutritionSolver entries include approximately:

Hot Sauce: 1,650mg sodium

Medium Sauce: 1,630mg

Original Buffalo: 1,480mg

Blazin' Knockout: 1,370mg

The final sodium total can increase further when you add fries, dips, cheese, or other sides.

If sodium matters to you, calculate the complete meal , not only the base wings.

Ranch and Bleu Cheese Count Too

Dipping sauce is easy to overlook because it comes in a relatively small container.

However, ranch and bleu cheese dressings can add their own calories and fat to a wing order.

If you use the full cup, include the full serving. If you only use part of it, your actual intake will be lower than the listed amount for the complete portion.

The same principle applies to any extra side sauce.

Fries and Wing Bundles Should Be Calculated as Meals

Buffalo Wild Wings currently sells bundles such as bone-in or boneless wings with fries, along with larger wing combinations.

When a bundle includes fries, do not assume the wing nutrition already tells you the total unless the calculator specifically lists the complete bundle as one verified entry.

Where individual values are available, calculating:

wings + flavor + fries + dipping sauce

gives you much clearer information.

Buffalo Wild Wings Is More Than Wings

The current menu also includes burgers, chicken sandwiches, wraps, appetizers, sides, desserts, and other items.

If your order includes a burger or sandwich with fries, use the complete meal builder in the same way.

Protein can be useful when comparing entrées, but look at calories, fat, carbs, and sodium alongside it rather than judging the meal by protein alone.
